# Changelog

Format: each version is `## [X.Y.Z]` (with ` - YYYY-MM-DD` once it is published) and has two sections:
`### Players` (what players notice) and `### Technical` (internal changes). The top version
matches `mod_version` in `gradle.properties`; `tools/changelog.py` checks it and extracts the release notes.

## [0.1.0]

### Players

- **The Herald's House stands on its own land inside the wall too, and has trees** (towns placed from now on): the town no longer keeps a lawn behind its wall whatever the land. On a desert its ground is sand, on badlands red sand, on stony land gravel, in a swamp mud; the crops keep their irrigated plots, and on sand the flower beds become a dry garden (bare sand with a dead bush here and there). On grassland it is the lawn it was. And between the buildings there are now real trees, two dozen oaks and birches on the widest lawns, each in its own pit of soil, low hedges at the foot of the houses and potted plants among them. Trees burn, as trees do, but the town does not: nothing that burns indoors is within reach of a fire that lightning starts in a tree or a hedge. Nobody's walk changes: nothing is planted on a street, a lane or a door's way out. **In an existing world, the town already placed stays exactly as it is.**
- **The Herald's House no longer leaves its own wall full of water where the town cuts into a pond** (towns placed from now on): a wall, a step, a slab or a lantern of the town that fell inside a pond's water was built with that water inside it. The town took the water that spilled out while it was being built away again, so the finished town looked dry; but the wall itself stayed full, and the first block anyone placed or broke beside it set it pouring down the hillside for good. The town's own pieces — the house, the huts along its roads and the houses of its grounds — are now built dry, and the pond beside the town is drained or banked exactly as before. The dock is unchanged: its posts stand in the water on purpose. **In an existing world, the town already placed stays as it is.**
- **The houses of a village are no longer one house twelve times** (villages generated from now on): inside each style the houses kept the style's materials but shared one box and one roof; the four small ones were identical. Now a third of a hip-roofed style's houses have a gable and a fifth of a gabled style's a hip, the ridge runs along the street on some and across it on others, hip roofs get chimneys too, some long gables a dormer, the flat-roofed houses of the deserts and badlands a roof room, a wool shade or a bare terrace, stone walls are damp low and cracked high in patches, and half the households leave firewood or cut stone by the door. Beds, doors, workstations and rooms are where they were. **In an existing world, villages already generated stay as they are.**
- **Round the Herald's House, every building is its own** (towns, roads and grounds placed from now on): the eight roadside huts were one flat-roofed cube with eight awning colours; each now has its own walls (planks, a log cabin, white daub, mud brick, stone, brick, cobble), its own roof (a gable along the road or facing it, a hip, a single slope), an awning or a slab porch, something of its trade on the porch and, two of them, a chimney. The five houses of the grounds were one gabled box in five materials; they now have five roofs (a porch, a dormer, a hip, a terrace behind a parapet with beams, a gable end to the yard). Inside the town, the thin dirt threads from each door to its street are worn cobbled lanes with a verge that fades into the grass. **In an existing world, what is already placed stays as it is**; a hut or a house placed later (more huts allowed in the configuration, `/heraldia herald roads` or `grounds` in a world that had none) is one of the new ones.
- **A village's banners no longer skip a mossy wall** (every village, old and new): the decorations a village puts up for a player it likes (the towers' and the gate's banners and lanterns, the shutters of a village that shuns you) only go on the village's own blocks, and a wall block that the village's weathering had turned mossy or cracked as it generated was taken for a block that was not its own: about one stone block in eight, so a cobblestone watchtower was left with a single banner about one time in four. A weathered block of the village's own wall now counts as its wall. **In an existing world**, a village that was missing a decoration for this reason gets it the next time it shows its look; nothing is taken down, and a block a player placed is still never decorated.
- **No stream from nowhere beside the Herald's House** (towns placed from now on): where the town cut a pond or a lake open, the water that had already run out of it down the hillside stayed for good after the pond was drained, a stream with no source, and the gates' roads laid plank walkways over it. That water is now taken away with the pond, and the road is paved over the dry ground as it would have been. **In an existing world, the town already placed stays as it is.**
- **The hermit sleeps indoors** (Hermitages generated from now on): the hermit's cell gets its own door, in the chapel's south wall. With the north door alone, a hermit who came home from the south stopped against the cell's wall, his bed two blocks away through it and some thirty on foot round the chapel, and often did not get in: on a server, 5 times in 8 from the yard south of the chapel; with the door, 16 in 16 from the same places. **A hermitage already generated stays as it is**: break a doorway into the cell yourself if its hermit sleeps outside.
- **The innkeeper sleeps indoors** (Roadside Inns generated from now on): the inn's bedroom gets its own door to the yard. With the street door alone, an innkeeper who strolled behind the house sometimes could not find his way back to bed (it was a few steps away through the wall and a long walk round the house), gave it up and spent his nights outside. The chest moves to the corner of the room. **An inn already generated stays as it is**: break a doorway into the back room yourself if its innkeeper sleeps outside.
- **The Wharf Guard stays at its bollard** (Fallen Shulker Wharfs of the End midlands and barrens generated from now on): on that style of the wharf the guard shulker sat on an end stone brick wall, which a shulker cannot hold on to, so it let go the moment the wharf appeared and teleported to anywhere within eight blocks, under the pier included; the wharf's guard was never at its post. Its post is now a full purpur pillar, as on the highlands style, where it always stayed. **In an existing world**, a wharf already generated keeps its wall bollard and its guard wherever it went.
- **The Herald's House sits in its land and has a skyline** (towns placed from now on): the town no longer lays a rectangle of lawn on a desert. The ground round its wall, the forecourt outside the south gate and the plots of the houses of its grounds keep the land's own surface (sand on sand, terracotta on badlands, grass only where there was grass), and where the town stands above lower land its foundation shows as the wall's masonry instead of bare stone. The wall is no longer a ruler: pilasters carried up into taller merlons, a corbel course under the battlements, an arrow loop in every bay outside and a blind arch with a buttress in every bay inside, moss low and cracks high in patches, and two roofed towers on each side that the wall walk passes through. The gatehouse towers get machicolations and tiled roofs. Every roof gets eaves, a ridge and patches of a kin material, the long ones dormers, the wide hipped ones a little lantern on top. Nothing else moves: the same buildings, doors, beds, residents and lights. **In an existing world, the town already placed stays exactly as it is.**
- **The Herald's House grounds are terraced, not stepped, and no water stands in the air** (worlds whose town is placed from now on): on a hillside, the protected ring round the town was levelled into dozens of one-block steps one after another ("rice terraces"); ground that climbs that fast is now gathered into terraces about 5 blocks wide, each a few blocks over the next with a stone retaining wall (cobblestone on grass, sandstone on sand, and so on), and the ground still meets the roads, huts, houses, water and the natural edge walking. A pond or a lake whose ground the town or its grounds cut away no longer leaves a wall of still water hanging in the air: a small one drains, a bigger one gets a bank. And the town's roads no longer step on steep land (the owner's spawn had 27 steps): each road's heights are worked out whole, climbing early enough. **In an existing world**, a town, roads and grounds already built stay exactly as they are.
- **Look, but do not take, in the Herald's House** (on by default; `casa_heraldo.contenedores_solo_ver`): the chests, barrels, kegs, furnaces and every other container of the town and its protected ring, the grounds' houses included, now open for every player, who sees what they really hold (their loot rolls as usual); a player who is not an operator cannot take or put a single item, whatever they click, drag or press. An operator, a tenant inside their own stall, the owner of a return barrel and everyone's own ender chest work as before; a stall someone else rents stays shut. Turned off, the containers stay shut as before.
- **No empty chest anywhere** (new structures only: chunks generated from now on): every chest, barrel and dispenser of every Heraldia structure now has something inside when you open it, even if it is little. The 2,843 containers that came out empty (the kegs of the inns and taverns, the crates of the markets and the streets, the barrels of the houses, mines, ships, lighthouses, the citadel's storerooms, Atlantis's ruins, the rooms of the Herald's House...) get a **poor filler** that fits the place: bread and vegetables in a pantry, bottles and sugar in a keg, coal, nuggets and worn stone tools in a smithy, cod and kelp at a fisherman's, arrows and string in a storeroom, coal, torches and some rail in a mine, chorus fruit in an End outpost. One to three cheap stacks: no emeralds, diamonds, enchantments or treasure fragments, and they **never restock** and are not raised by the difficulty, so looting a village is not a living and the structures' own chests keep their worth. Thirteen containers stay empty on purpose: the Herald's House's 12 **return barrels** (the market keeps a former tenant's things in them, only while they are empty) and the dispenser inside the palace's throne column (a part of the secret door's circuit). **In an existing world**, containers already generated stay as they are: an empty one stays empty; only chunks generated after the update get the filler (the Herald's House is built once, so an existing town keeps its empty rooms).
- **The Herald's House grounds** (new; a new placement builds them on its own, after its roads): the protected ring around the town (50 blocks on each side) is levelled into gentle, walkable ground that keeps the shape of the land, with no step of more than a block, no loose column and no hole, meeting the town at its wall and the natural ground at its edge (never cut or filled by more than 10 blocks: a cliff too high keeps a step, and the log says how many columns were too steep). On it stand **a few cherry trees** (up to 10, never by lava or fire) and **small houses** of five designs (8 by default, never flammable), each with a door onto a little front yard with flowers and a lantern, furniture and **a chest with a few modest things in it** (a filler loot table: never empty, not restocked); no villagers live there and nothing in them is a bed or a workstation. The roads, their lamps and huts, water, structures and players' blocks are never touched, the gates' roads never cross a house and the arena road goes round them (in about 1 % of worlds, when every detour crosses a structure, it reaches one and leaves its columns as they are). It is all inside the protected area, under its rules. New options `casa_heraldo.alrededores.activado` (on) and `casa_heraldo.alrededores.casas` (8, from 0 to 12), with their texts in the 8 languages. **In an existing world**, a town already standing is never touched on its own: an operator builds its grounds with the new `/heraldia herald grounds` (once; a second run builds nothing).
- **Roads out of the Herald's House** (new; a new placement builds them on its own): from each of the town's four gates a road goes out past the wall, at least 45 blocks beyond the gate (72 with its bend), mostly moving away from spawn: 5 wide and built as one work: one paving with a kerb on each edge (a dirt path edged with coarse dirt; smooth sandstone edged with cut sandstone on sand; gravel edged with cobblestone on stone and snow; plank bridges and walkways over short water), level from side to side, climbing slopes in regular one-block steps, with four blocks of clear air over it (a tree on the road or beside it is felled whole, leaves and all) and a tidy shoulder that keeps its grass and flowers, lit by **lamp posts with lanterns every 7 blocks** (no monster spawns on it: every road column stays within light of a lantern), never through a village, a mine, a medium or unique structure or the arena. With these roads on, the town's own dirt path across its protected ring and the signpost at its end are no longer drawn (the gates' roads are the road there: a straight path beside the road's bend would have shown as a stub); instead each gate's road carries a sign by its end pointing back to the Herald's House with its distance. With `casa_heraldo.caminos.activado` off, the ring's path and its signpost are drawn as before, and now really show: until now the path went under the grass and the signpost was never placed. By the roads, near the town, stand **humble traders' huts** (cobblestone, planks, log posts and a striped wool awning over the porch), each with a villager who trades with you (vanilla trades: farmer, fisherman, shepherd, fletcher, leatherworker, mason, butcher), its bed, its workstation and its own bell on the porch. **The road towards the wave arena is built all the way to its entrance**, round the arena if it has to, with a sign at each end; at the town's exit stands the hut of **Garcés, warden of the road**, a weaponsmith who tells you, in the choice window (never the chat), that the road leads to the arena where the finest warriors fight, with a little of its story ([Tell me more]), and sells his blades. If the arena is placed after the town, its road and the guide's hut are built then. New options `casa_heraldo.caminos.activado` (on) and `casa_heraldo.caminos.aldeanos` (5: the guide and one per road; at most 8, the guide included), with their texts in the 8 languages. **In an existing world**, a town already standing is never touched on its own: an operator builds its roads with the new `/heraldia herald roads` (only what is missing; a second run builds nothing).
- **The Herald's House, rebuilt as a capital** (new placements only): the spawn town grows to **128 × 128** (protected area 228 × 228) and gets a silhouette: a thick wall with a wall walk, corner towers with copper cones and banners, a **gatehouse** in the south opening onto a forecourt and an approach avenue with banners, a **clock tower** over the plaza (a real clock on each face, the job board in its loggia), a raised dais with steps under a copper canopy for the bell, the **monument to the Herald**, flagpoles and lamp posts, and a **town hall** with a balcony over the plaza. Every building has its own design and furniture: a two-storey inn with hearth, long tables, bar and kegs; a chapel with rose window and pews; a library with bookshelf walls and a reading nook; a smithy with a smoking chimney, forge, anvil yard and coal bin; a butcher with a striped awning and meat hanging in frames; a cartographer with maps on the walls; a bakery with its oven, a herbalist, a workshop, a weaver, a stable with horses in their stalls, a granary by the farm and a guardhouse. **18 villagers** live and work there (each with a bed, a workstation and a bell near home), two iron golems keep the night watch on the walls, chickens peck in their run, cats sit on the roofs, chimneys smoke, laundry hangs, a cart and crates stand by the market, and the village events (market days, weddings, funerals, festivals) can now happen on its plaza. Nothing in it burns: no flammable wood, and the few flammable furnishings sit where a lightning fire cannot reach them. **In an existing world**, a town already placed stays exactly as it is (the 96 × 96 one, with its protection, stalls and barrels where they were); a town placed from now on, by `/heraldia herald place`, is the new one.
- **Traces of work in progress in the villages** (new): a few plots among the houses (about one lot in twenty) show that someone lives there and was busy a moment ago: a house half built with its scaffolding and the walls rising unevenly, laundry hanging out, a meal left half eaten at a table in the open, a work yard with the tools on the ground (a carpenter's or a stonemason's), a cart loaded with the family's goods, fish drying on racks or a cooking fire with the pot hanging over it. Each of the 36 styles only draws what suits its climate and character (no laundry or picnic in the snow, fish racks on the coast and in the swamp...), in its own materials. Blocks only: no entity, no bed, no workstation and no loot, so it costs nothing while playing. New option `aldeas.vida.rastros` (on; off, those plots stay as empty plots of grass or sand), with its texts in the 8 languages. **In an existing world, new villages only**; the villages generated from this version have a slightly different layout from the same seed (one more kind of lot to draw).
- **Talk to the villagers** (new, also in villages already generated): a right click on a Heraldia villager inside a Heraldia village first opens a conversation in chat (vanilla clients too, in each player's language): a greeting and a line by its profession, the village's land, the moment of the day, the weather and your reputation with the village, with buttons to **[Keep talking]**, ask **[Any news?]**, **[Trade]** (its trades, as always) and **[Goodbye]**; the same line does not come up twice in a row. **Sneak + right click goes straight to the trades**, and vanilla villagers, those of other mods, the bailiff, Carpet bots and trading automation's fake players trade as always. Each villager rests a minute between conversations (`dialogos.espera_segundos`). **One in ten has travelled** (`dialogos.viajeros_porcentaje`): asked for news by a player the village knows (`dialogos.pista_reputacion`, 20), it tells, once in its life, in which direction and roughly how far a Heraldia structure that really exists lies (never coordinates). `dialogos.activado` turns it all off; operators can preview a conversation with `/heraldia talk with <villager>`. The lines are written in each of the 8 languages (195 profession lines and 246 village lines in every one), composed in each language rather than translated from another. **In an existing world**, every Heraldia villager in its village talks from now on; nothing generated changes.
- **Conversations and the arena's mode menu in the middle of the screen, not in the chat** (also in existing worlds): talking to a Heraldia villager, or ringing the arena's bell, now opens a window in the middle of the screen, the way a chest does (the mouse is released, the world dims a little behind it, you choose by clicking or with the keyboard: number keys, arrows, Enter, Escape). With Heraldia on your client you get a speech panel with the speaker's name, the line and the options as buttons; with a vanilla client, a chest window whose glass panes are the options (the line is on the paper and in each pane's description; nothing in it can be taken or moved, and your inventory is left exactly as it was). After [Goodbye], the farewell stays on screen with [Close]. A wayfarer's hint arrives in the same window. `dialogos.en_pantalla` (on) set to `false` brings back the chat buttons for everyone; `/heraldia talk more|rumor|trade|bye` still work and answer in chat. Nothing changes in the lines, the rest between conversations, the hints, sneak + right click (straight to the trades) or the villagers that do not talk. **In an existing world**, nothing saved changes: new option with its default, no new saved data.
- **The villagers speak your language again**: a player in Spanish (by their client or with `/heraldia language es_es`) heard the villagers' lines, in conversations and in the village events, in English; they now hear the Spanish ones, as every other language already did. **In an existing world**, nothing saved changes.
- **The Chronicle has an end** (new): when you have defeated the guardians of every unique structure of the main world (Atlantis included; 20 today), the last oath breaks and Ysolda's **last leaf** reaches you, a book that closes the story; you become a **Herald of Atlantis** (a challenge advancement announced in chat and shown on screen, and your title under your name in `/heraldia journal`) and get the **Standard of the Heralds**, an epic banner with the Order's arms that you can raise anywhere. The Nether and End guardians are optional: defeating them too gives **Beyond the Map** and one more page in the last leaf. A guardian counts for you if you landed the killing blow (also before this version: your guardian advancement says so), if it fell with you within 64 blocks (everyone in a group fight counts), or if it is the only one in the world (Atlantis) and it has already fallen to anyone. `/heraldia journal` shows a new line with the guardians defeated, and `/heraldia journal chronicle` names those defeated and those still keeping watch (never where); once closed, `/heraldia journal chronicle book` gives a copy of the last leaf. Per player, in the 8 languages, with vanilla clients. New option `guia.final_cronica` (on by default). **In an existing world**, players who had already defeated every guardian get the end the next time they join; those who only fought next to the killer before this version are not credited with those guardians (an operator can grant the guardian advancement); every fight from now on counts for everyone in it. Server only.
- **Villages that move on their own**: the villagers of the new Heraldia villages now walk the village during the day, with no extra entity and nothing running every tick (only where beds, workstations and the bell stand: vanilla AI does the rest). Nobody works next to their own bed any more: each house holds another trade's workstation, and the smith and the farmer sleep in a house, so every morning they cross the streets and the avenues to the smithy, the farm, the market or a neighbour's workshop, gather at the bell on the square at midday and go back home at nightfall. Every bed can be reached on foot: the 2-storey houses keep it on the ground floor (upstairs, a loft with the chest) and the inns and taverns climb to their rooms by a staircase instead of a ladder; their rooms always have a bed, for the priest, the librarian and the other masters, who work in the special buildings and sleep elsewhere. Measured on a server over one in-game day (8 villages of 8 styles, standard and large, about 100 villagers, flat world): villagers on the move 84 → 92, still 15 → 8, walking between home and work 34 → 50, working under 6 blocks from their bed 24 → 12, gathering at the bell 61 → 77; the same share sleeps in a bed at night. **In an existing world**, villages already generated keep their placement; only new ones change.
- **Villages that fit the terrain** (new villages only): on hills, the streets of a new Heraldia village no longer have steps a villager cannot climb: a street with one is graded when the village is created (a gentle slope, at most a block a column and at most 6 blocks of fill or cut, shaped by the game's own terrain adaptation, so the village is not sunk into a hollow), and a door two or three blocks off its street gets a short ramp; a house whose door is further off, or a garden or well sunk in a pit, is left out (one house less instead of one nobody can use). No trees, fallen trees or bamboo grow on the village's pieces any more: in forests and jungles they closed streets and doors. Measured over one in-game day on 16 naturally generated villages of two seeds (~220 villagers): villagers with a bed 61 → 65 %, on the move 88 → 90 % (91 % on a flat world), asleep at night 52 → 57 %, at the bell 45 → 52 %. **In an existing world**, villages already generated keep their terrain and their trees; only villages created from now on fit the terrain. With `/place`, which does not adapt the terrain, only the houses left out apply.
- **Wayfarers** (new, also in existing worlds, only along the roads that exist): now and then, with a player nearby, someone passes along a Heraldia road, as fits the area's village family: a **pilgrim** walking with its staff towards the nearest monastery or cathedral (only if one really spawns within 3,000 blocks), a **shepherd** driving 3 to 5 sheep in the colors of the land to the next village (not in desert, badlands, jungle or swamp), a **messenger** running with a letter who never stops, a **pair of guards** (two iron golems of the village of origin) patrolling, also by night, or a **hunter** coming back with the area's game (rabbit, boar or fowl) and its dog, a tame wolf of the local variant. The players nearby read in the action bar who passes and where it goes. The shepherd (wool, mutton, buys wheat) and the hunter (cooked game, hides, arrows, a rabbit's foot) trade, cheap and few; the pilgrim, the messenger and the guards do not. Hurting one costs reputation with its village of origin, as with its villagers and golems; the guards answer together and the dog defends its owner. No reward and nothing drops: they are life on the road. Options `viajeros.activado`, `viajeros.frecuencia` (30 %, one draw per minute, 2 minutes of rest), `viajeros.max_activos` (1) and one switch per kind (`viajeros.<kind>.activado`), all reloadable; operators bring one with `/heraldia caravan wayfarer [pilgrim | shepherd | messenger | patrol | hunter]`, and `/heraldia caravan list` and `clear` include them. **In an existing world**, nothing generated changes: they only walk the roads already there, in loaded chunks, and leave no entity behind.
- **Premium chest of a cleared unique**: with restocking loot on, the premium chest of a unique whose guardian has already fallen no longer refills with its full premium (with the spawners off, that was premium loot again and again with no danger at all). Its refills now bring only 1-2 emeralds 40 % of the time and, 8 % of the time, one modest enchanted book (enchanting-table enchantments, levels 5-15). The first opening is untouched (the reward for beating the structure), and refills while the guardian lives are the full premium as before; the other chests of the structure keep their own loot. New option `botin.reponer.premium_vencido_modesto` (on by default; `false` restocks the full premium as before), in the "Loot" section of the panel. **In an existing world**, it applies to uniques beaten from the previous change on (those recorded as cleared); a unique whose guardian died before keeps restocking its full premium. Server only.
- **Ambience** (new, also in existing worlds), with the game's own sounds and particles, so vanilla clients hear it and nothing new is downloaded: the **village bell** rings three strikes at sunrise and at nightfall for whoever is inside a Heraldia village; in **rain or a thunderstorm** lighthouses sound a deep **siren** that carries out to sea (within 160 blocks, in the open); in the **galleries of the mines** a drop falls from the ceiling every 20-45 s and, rarely, the cave's echo is heard. Each sound reaches only the players it is meant for, in the Ambient/Environment sound category, with a cooldown per player (bell twice a day at most, siren every 3 min, echo every 4 min). One option per effect (`ambiente.campanas`, `ambiente.sirena_faro`, `ambiente.minas`) and `ambiente.activado` for all of them, reloadable with `/heraldia reload`. **In an existing world**, they play in the villages, lighthouses and mines already generated; nothing is saved in the world. The tavern song of the Corsair's Isle was left out on purpose (see [Ambience](docs/CONTENT.md#ambience)).
- **No more stall on the first `/locate` after a restart**: the answers of structure searches are now kept in the world and served from there, so `/locate`, the treasure maps (cartographers and chests), the Chronicle folios and `/heraldia info nearby` no longer recompute on the server thread what they already worked out in an earlier session. Measured after a restart, from 8000, -8000: mine entrances 1.1-1.5 s → 34-51 ms, villages 164-179 → 26-35 ms, the castle 126-136 → 1-2 ms, medium structures 168-183 → 4-6 ms; lighthouses and wrecks, up to 9 s → 17 ms; a search that finds nothing, 1.6 s → 0.3-0.5 s (also the first time). **Exactly the same places** as before. **In an existing world**, nothing changes in what is generated; the memory starts empty and fills as players search, and it is thrown away on its own if the seed, a generation option (also through a changed default), a mod, a datapack or the mod's `.jar` changes ([details](docs/CONFIGURATION.md#search-memory)).
- **Cleared uniques**: when the guardian of a unique structure falls, the spawners of that whole structure turn off for good (their cages stay, empty): the conquered structure is left clean and safe to live in, and is no longer an endless experience farm. Also the spawners in parts of the structure that were not loaded at that moment, as soon as they load. New option `jefes.apagar_generadores` (on by default; `false` keeps them working as before), in the "Combat" section of the panel. **In an existing world**, uniques beaten from this version on turn off on their own; those whose guardian died before stay as they were (their spawners can still be broken with a pickaxe), because guessing it from the missing guardian could turn off a structure nobody has beaten. Server only.
- **Your journal** (new): `/heraldia journal` (any player, also with vanilla clients, in the 8 languages) shows in chat what you have found and what you are still missing, kind by kind with a progress bar and the overall total: village families, unique structures, medium structures by type, mine entrances by size, the coast (lighthouse and shipwreck) and the Nether and the End. Each line opens its kind (`/heraldia journal uniques`...) with the names found and missing; hovering over it lists what is missing. No coordinates and no clues: the Herald's Guide keeps the biome clues of the missing unique structures, and the journal points to it. Read from your own advancements: nothing new is saved. `/heraldia status` points each player to their journal, and it now also counts the Nether and End medium structures as discovered (before, they never did). **In an existing world**, it counts everything players already discovered (their advancements are already there).
- **Medium structures stay dense by default**: `medianas.frecuencia_porcentaje` keeps **100** (among all types, one every ~550 blocks; on a straight walk you pass one every ~1.9 km), for a world full of structures, also at short range. A development build lowered it to 60 for a few hours (one every ~610 blocks); its own measurement showed the density costs nothing measurable in world generation (inside the noise at every value from 100 to 60), so it went back to 100. Fewer are still an option (`"medianas": {"frecuencia_porcentaje": 60}`; the light profile uses 50). **In a world started with that 60**, nothing already generated changes; new chunks get 100, so there may be a seam between the land explored before and after, and the mod warns at startup (log and operators' chat: `medianas.frecuencia_porcentaje 60 → 100`); writing 60 in the file by hand keeps that world as it was, without a warning. Figures in `docs/CONFIGURATION.md`, "Medium structures density".
- **Light profile** (new value `ligero` of the `perfil` option, for modest machines and servers with little RAM): the same game as `estandar` with lighter world generation: rarer large villages (10 % instead of 25 %), no roads and no caravans, fewer and more spread-out medium structures (frequency 50 %, 320 blocks apart) and vanilla mineshafts at vanilla frequency (×1 instead of ×2). It is applied whole like the other profiles (what is written by hand in the file overrides it and makes it `personalizado`), and it is in `/heraldia profile`, in the in-game panel (`/heraldia config`, a fourth button), on the client's screen and in the 8 languages. The other profiles still never touch generation. **In an existing world**, nothing already generated changes: only the chunks generated after the restart follow it, and next to the land already explored there are seams (a dense zone next to an emptier one, roads ending at the edge, medium structures that `/locate` no longer finds); choosing it in the panel in a world already played asks for confirmation first, and at every start the mod warns (log and operators' chat) when the generation options changed since the previous start. On a **first start** (no profile in `config/heraldia.json`) the mod looks at the memory given to Java and the cores: with less than 3.5 GB or 2 cores or fewer it suggests the light profile in the log and to each operator, with a button to apply it; it never applies it on its own and stays quiet once a profile is chosen.
- Changing the profile from the in-game panel (`/heraldia config`) now really changes it: before, the options kept the previous profile's values, written as set by hand, and the new profile came out as `personalizado`. **In an existing world**, a file already saved that way keeps its values (they are set by hand); choose the profile again in the panel to fix it.
- **Things happen in the villages** (new, also in villages already generated): now and then, while you are in a Heraldia village, its square holds an event for a few minutes, and you see it from fifty blocks away: a **market day** in the morning (two to four stalls with striped awnings and their goods on the counter, a trader behind each one with real trades of the village's produce, shoppers in front), a **wedding** in fine weather (a white and pink arch at the head of a carpet aisle, the couple and the cleric under it, the guests in two rows with flowers, children at the back, the bell, a firework when it starts and three when they say yes), a **funeral** (a bier under a black pall between two soul lanterns, the cleric, the widow and the mourners with candles, the bell tolling slowly) or a **festival** at dusk (a bonfire, two maypoles, a band of three playing a folk tune with notes over their heads and a ring of dancers; the host sells cookies, cake, honey and fireworks). Players within 160 blocks are told it has begun; the figures speak through the villagers' lines (the ceremony, the eulogy, greetings, calls, in the 8 languages), greet you when you come close and you can take part: buy at the market, give the couple or the widow a gift (flowers, an emerald, a cake...), ask the band for another song; each gives a little reputation with the village, once per event. When it ends everything goes. One event per village and at most `eventos.max_activos` (2) on the server; `eventos.frecuencia` (20 % per draw) and `eventos.espera_minutos` (20) make them rarer; off with `eventos.activado` and with the light profile. **In an existing world**, events only ever place blocks on air in a free cell of the square (never on a player's block, the job board's surroundings or the reputation decorations' spots) and only take down what is still exactly what they put up: a square a player has built on gets a smaller event or none.

- **Safe updates** (permanent rule: no version changes what is already built without the administrator's permission). **In-game configuration panel** for operators, without the mod on the client: `/heraldia config` opens a dialog with the game profile at the top and seven sections (World and generation, Villages and roads, Combat and bosses, Loot, Arena, Players and Guide, Advanced) of 5 to 8 options, each with a sentence explaining it, the recommended value and a warning if it applies on restart; saving applies it like `/heraldia reload`. From the console, `/heraldia config set <option> <value>`. **Per-player language**: `/heraldia language` (for everyone) chooses the language of Heraldia's texts, which overrides the client's. **Bringing new content to an existing world**: `/heraldia update analyze` (report, text and image map; changes nothing) and `/heraldia update apply` (with confirmation, optional backup, server stop and work at startup; `/heraldia update undo` to undo): it regenerates untouched chunks and places new Heraldia structures only in safe spots, always more than **50 blocks** (`actualizar.margen_bloques`) from any detected player build (player blocks, differences with the regeneration, InhabitedTime, beds, respawn, spawn). **Per-version repairs** that apply on their own once when each mob loads: 0.1.0 guardians without marks are anchored (they stop wandering out of their room and picking up items) and become bosses; baby guardians become adults; drowned riding zombie nautiluses are dismounted. `/heraldia update repair [dry-run]` forces them on what is loaded. **In an existing world, this is exactly what it is for: nothing changes until the administrator asks, except the mob repairs.**

- **Wizard's huts** (new, one more kind of medium structure): 9 × 9 huts with a steep roof in a 17 × 17 yard, richly decorated: bookshelves, a lectern with the wizard's notes, a counter with brewing stands, amethyst and candles, cauldrons, a chimney with a campfire and flue, and a loft with a bed, cobwebs and a star map on the gables; outside, a garden of herbs and mushrooms with the wizard's cat or a circle of stones with candles and amethyst. The wizard (with a name of their own, such as "Morwen of the Mist") sells bottles, lapis lazuli, potions, a modest enchanted book and ender pearls. For operators, `/heraldia wizard` (nearby wizards and whether they are evil) and `/heraldia wizard reveal` (reveals the nearest one, for testing). - **Arena modes and weekly leaderboard** (new): when you ring the bell you choose the mode in chat (one button per mode; with only one enabled, it starts as always): **co-op** (the usual one, unchanged), **time attack** (10 waves, `arena.modos.oleadas_contrarreloj`, against the clock, with short breaks; it ends on its own and scores the time) and **survival** (waves without breaks until everyone falls; scores the highest wave). The usual rules in all three (no loot, 15 % experience, inventory kept, a mini-boss every 5 waves, difficulty and profile). Each mode has its **weekly leaderboard** (`/heraldia arena leaderboard [coop|timeattack|survival [weeks ago]]`, any player) and its all-time record; the week closes on Monday at 00:00, server time (`arena.temporada.dia_reinicio` and `arena.temporada.hora_reinicio`; if the server was off, at startup) or with `/heraldia arena season reset` (operators): its champions are announced in chat, earn their advancement and appear in the **hall of fame**, three signs on the arena promenade next to the entrance (they update themselves) and two new pages of the Herald's Guide (champions and records). The last 8 weeks are kept (`arena.temporada.historico`). Six new advancements (107 in the tab, with the caravan ones): finish a 10-wave time attack, do it in under 15 minutes, pass wave 15 in survival and win a week in each mode. New options `arena.modos.cooperativo`, `contrarreloj`, `supervivencia` and `oleadas_contrarreloj` and `arena.temporada.*`, reloadable with `/heraldia reload`; `/heraldia arena start` accepts the mode (and any player can use it from the ring: these are the bell's buttons). Texts in the 8 languages. **In an existing world**: the already placed arena gets its signs if their spot is free (if something is there, it is not touched) and the scoreboard record becomes the co-op record.
- **Treasure hunt** (new): sometimes a chest in a medium structure, a mine entrance or a unique structure (and the pay of the job board's quests and feats) holds a **treasure map fragment**. Right-clicking deciphers it and it becomes a vanilla map with a mark: a nearby medium structure; there, right-click again, and the map moves on to the next mark, a mine entrance; and from there, to the spot where Ysolda buried her chest. The map says what to look for, in which biome and in which direction; used far away, how many blocks away it is. The chest is buried when you arrive, always in natural ground and never inside, on top of or next to anything players have built (if someone built on the spot, the mark moves a little). It holds **Ysolda's Compass** (a named keepsake) and good loot without breaking progression. Each map is completed only once (a copy does not give another chest either) and keeps its progress across restarts. Two new advancements (A Piece of a Map and Ysolda's Treasure; 100 in the tab), a new page in the Herald's Guide and texts in the 8 languages. New options `tesoro.activado`, `tesoro.probabilidad` (3 % per chest) and `tesoro.radio_max` (1500 blocks), in the "Treasure hunt" section of the configuration screen and reloadable with `/heraldia reload`; none of them changes generation. New operator command `/heraldia treasure give|info`. **Also in an existing world**: the marks are chosen among the already generated structures (and, if there are none, among those that will appear in unexplored land, saying so), without generating anything while searching.
- **Caravans** (new, also in existing worlds, only along the roads that exist): every now and then, with players nearby, a Heraldia Merchant with 1 to 3 llamas (and sometimes a guard golem) travels about 128 blocks of the road between two neighboring villages. Sometimes (`caravanas.prob_asalto`, 35 %; 15 % on casual and 60 % on hardcore) 2 to 4 bandits from the bandit camp raid it; if you defend it and it arrives, it pays emeralds (and an item) and reputation with the destination village, with the **Safe Road** advancement. If the merchant dies, it is lost without leaving loot. Options `caravanas.activado`, `caravanas.frecuencia`, `caravanas.prob_asalto` and `caravanas.max_activas` (reloadable with `/heraldia reload`); for operators, `/heraldia caravan spawn [raid | peaceful] | raid | list | clear`. A new page in the Guide and a line in `/heraldia status`. Advancements tab: 101.
- **Job board quests and per-village reputation** (new, also in already generated villages): when you touch the lectern of the square's job board (or with `/heraldia quest` inside the village), 2 to 4 quests appear in chat, with buttons, that change every few days (`encargos.rotacion_dias`, 3 by default): bring items (food, biome materials or moderate ores), defeat the guardian of a specific unique structure, explore a medium structure or a mine entrance (with a heading and approximate distance, without coordinates), carry a message to a neighboring village along the road or pass wave N of the arena. Several players can take the same one; each carries up to 5 (`/heraldia quest list`, `accept`, `abandon`, `turnin`). They pay emeralds, experience and reputation with that village, which drops when you attack its villagers or golems; as known, friend and hero of the village, its villagers discount trades by 10, 20 and 30 % (like Hero of the Village, not stacking), greet you and the board shows your title. Four new advancements (98 in total), a new page in the Herald's Guide (17), and `/heraldia status` counts the ones turned in. Options `encargos.activado`, `encargos.rotacion_dias` and `reputacion.activado`, applied with `/heraldia reload`. Works with vanilla clients.

An option written in `config/heraldia.json` overrides the profile (the log, `/heraldia profile` and the configuration screen say that the profile is now custom and which options differ).
