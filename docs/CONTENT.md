## Contents

- **Huge villages**: 72 structures (36 biome styles, each in a standard and a large size).
- **Atlantis**: a single sunken city per world (see below).
- **Caravans**: merchants with llamas travelling the roads between villages; defend them from bandits (see [Caravans](#caravans)).
- **Wayfarers**: pilgrims, shepherds, messengers, pairs of guards and hunters passing along the roads near you, by area (see [Wayfarers](#wayfarers)).
- **Villager conversations**: talk to the villagers of Heraldia villages; one in ten has travelled and, if the village trusts you, tells you where a structure lies (see [Villager conversations](#villager-conversations)).
- **Village events**: now and then a village's square holds a market day, a wedding, a funeral or a festival for a few minutes; take part and it remembers you (see [Village events](#village-events)).
- **Wizard's huts**: a wizard with trades in a richly decorated hut; one in five is an evil wizard in disguise (see [Wizard's huts](#wizards-huts)).
- **Wave arena**: a coliseum near spawn to play in waves, with records and rewards (see [Wave arena](#wave-arena)).
- **Nether and End**: 7 medium structures and 2 unique structures beyond the portals (see [Nether and End](#nether-and-end)).

## Huge villages

By default, one village every 1000-2000 blocks (average spacing 1500), at most 100 per world (the ones closest to 0,0) and never within 10 chunks of a vanilla village. The standard version appears 3 times as often as the large one. All of this can be changed in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration).

| Family | Biomes | Styles (ID `heraldia:<style>` and `heraldia:<style>_grande`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plains | plains, sunflower plains, meadow | `llanuras_castellana`, `llanuras_gallega`, `llanuras_entramado` |
| Temperate forest | forest, flower forest, birch forest (and old growth), dappled forest | `bosque_lenadores`, `bosque_elfica`, `bosque_abadia` |
| Dark forest | dark forest, pale garden | `oscuro_gotica`, `oscuro_alquimistas`, `oscuro_fortificada` |
| Taiga | taiga, old growth taigas | `taiga_nordica`, `taiga_eslava`, `taiga_cazadores` |
| Snow | snowy plains and taiga, grove, ice spikes | `nieve_alpina`, `nieve_fortaleza`, `nieve_pastores` |
| Desert | desert | `desierto_andalusi`, `desierto_obelisco`, `desierto_caravanera` |
| Savanna | savanna, savanna plateau | `sabana_paja`, `sabana_terracota`, `sabana_pastores` |
| Jungle | jungle, sparse jungle, bamboo jungle | `jungla_arboles`, `jungla_templos`, `jungla_bambu` |
| Swamp | swamp, mangrove swamp | `pantano_palafitos`, `pantano_brujas`, `pantano_turba` |
| Cherry | cherry grove | `cerezos_japonesa`, `cerezos_templo`, `cerezos_artesanos` |
| Badlands | badlands, eroded and wooded badlands | `badlands_minera`, `badlands_roca`, `badlands_canon` |
| Coast | beach, snowy beach, stony shore | `costa_pescadores`, `costa_mercante`, `costa_mediterranea` |

Each village has:

- A main square with a fountain, benches, trees, a bell and two golems.
- Four avenues with the special buildings: a church or temple depending on the style (with cemetery and crypt), town hall, market, tavern, smithy, library, farm with windmill and inn. Each special building comes in three variants per style with a different floor plan and silhouette and the same function (for example the church with a front tower, with a transept and bell gable, or with a porch and the tower at the apse; the market open, as a covered hall or as an arcaded square), drawn at random for each village, so two villages of the same style differ in their main buildings. Sheets: [docs/aldeas/variantes.md](aldeas/variantes.md). On the coast there is also a harbor (harbor house and a pier with piles, crane and boats) on the free shore nearest to the square, with the pier facing the open sea; every shore within about 96 blocks along the coast is tried, and if only houses, fillers or towers stand in the way, the harbor takes their place. If no shore fits (they are all taken by the square, special buildings or streets reaching the sea, or there is no water nearby), the village has no harbor.
- About 20 houses in the standard size and about 30 in the large one, furnished, with chests in some and loot from common to epic (tables `heraldia:cofres/comun`, `poco_comun`, `raro`, `epico`).
- Watchtowers and wall gates at the ends of the streets; themed decoration between houses.
- Traces of work in progress (`aldeas.vida.rastros`, on): among the houses, a few plots (about one in twenty lots) show that someone lives there and was busy a moment ago, each drawn with the style's weights and in its materials: a **house half built** (the foundation laid, the walls rising unevenly, scaffolding and the blocks piled up), **laundry** hanging between two posts, a **meal left half eaten** at a table in the open (the cake half finished), a **work yard** with the tools on the ground (a carpenter's in the wooded and cold families, a stonemason's in the rest), a **cart loaded** with the family's goods (pumpkins, melons, pots, dried kelp...), **fish drying** on racks (coast, swamp, jungle and the Norse taiga) and a **cooking fire with the pot** hanging over it. Each style only gets what suits it: no laundry or picnic in the snow, no picnic in the dark forest or the abbey, no laundry in the gothic town, no carts in the temple town. Blocks only: no entity, no bed, no workstation, no bell and no loot. With the option off, those plots stay as empty plots of grass or sand. **In an existing world, new villages only.**
- Villagers: half the number of houses, over 80 % with a profession. Golems: at least one per 3 villagers.
- Village routes: the beds, workstations and the bell are placed so that the villagers walk the village on their own (vanilla AI, no extra entity or periodic work): nobody works next to their own bed (each house holds another trade's workstation, and the smith and the farmer sleep in a house), the priest, the town hall's cartographer, the librarian and the other masters of the special buildings work there and sleep in a house or in a room of the inn or the tavern (always with a bed, up a staircase), every bed can be reached on foot (the 2-storey houses keep it on the ground floor) and the bell stands on the square, where they gather at midday. Each morning they walk to work along the streets and the avenues, at midday to the square and at nightfall back home. **New villages only**: those already generated keep their placement.
- Fitting the terrain: villagers only claim a bed or a workstation they can walk to, and only look for a path a short way, so on hills every step they cannot climb costs beds. When a village is created, a street with such a step (two or more blocks from one column to the next) is **graded**: the ground under its centre line follows a gentle profile (at most a block a column, at most 6 blocks of fill or cut, as close to the terrain as that allows), and a door two or three blocks off its street gets a short ramp in front of it; the game's own terrain adaptation shapes the ground as the chunks are generated, so the village is not sunk into a hollow. A house whose door is further off its street, or a garden or well sunk in a pit it could not be left from, is not built (one house less instead of one nobody can use). Streets steeper than that are left as they are. And **no trees, fallen trees or bamboo grow on the village's pieces** (nor two blocks around them): in forests and jungles they used to close streets and doors. Measured on real terrain (the 8 villages nearest to the origin of the test seed, naturally generated): over one in-game day, the 8 villages nearest to the origin of two seeds (16 villages, about 220 villagers), villagers with a bed 61 → 65 %, on the move 88 → 90 % (91 % on a flat world), asleep in a bed at night 52 → 57 %, gathering at the bell 45 → 52 %; those same styles on a flat world reach 75 % with a bed, and the rest of the gap is mostly the two villages on mountains, with streets too steep to grade. **New villages only**: those already generated keep their terrain and their trees. With `/place`, which does not adapt the terrain, only the houses left out apply.

Population limits: there are about 1.3 beds per initial villager, so the population grows by 30 % at most.
The function `heraldia:golems` checks loaded villages every 30 s and removes one natural golem each time there are 8 or more (standard) or 9 or more (large); it does not touch player-built ones. Limits and cleanup can be changed in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration).

## Village life

Heraldia villages have trades with character, a job board and, in the fortified ones, sieges. Everything can be turned off in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration) (`aldeas.vida.*`).

- **Style trades** (`aldeas.vida.tratos_estilo`, on): in each style, one profession offers a trade with its character (vanilla items only, with a name, trims or moderate enchantments): the Militia Sword of the Castilian weaponsmith, the Copy of the Chronicle of the Heralds of the abbey librarian, the Dune Breastplate (sand-dyed leather with a gold trim) of the obelisk armorer, the Jarl's Mead of the Norse butcher, the Skipper's Rod of the fishermen... Besides, the cartographer of each family sells at level 3 (16 emeralds and a compass) the map of the unique structure of its area (castle in the plains, Jarl's Hall in the taiga, Stranded Galleon on the coast...). The trades are drawn like the vanilla ones when the villager levels up: a villager of that profession and level offers it with a probability of 1/2 to 2/3 (or always, at levels with a single vanilla trade), so a large village usually has one. Only Heraldia villagers inside a village of that style; also in villages already generated, for villagers that level up from now on.
- **Job board** (`aldeas.vida.tablon`, on): on the square, next to the fountain, a lectern with the book "Job Board" and, behind it, the **bailiff** (a named villager who neither moves nor takes damage) who pays for delivery jobs with his trades: *Harvest for the granary* (32 of the family's crop: wheat, potatoes, berries, cactus, cocoa... for 4 emeralds), *Firewood for winter* (32 logs of the style's wood for 3) and *Clear the roads* (16 rotten flesh and 8 bones for the Watch Cloak, a leather chestplate in the banner colour with Protection II). He has work for a few deliveries (no restocking). Deeds are paid by the village once per player when they get their advancement: *Night watch* (slay a threat inside a Heraldia village: 3 emeralds) and *Defend the village* (become Hero of the Village after a raid on a Heraldia village: 8 emeralds and the Defender's Horn). **New villages only.**
- **Quests and reputation** (`encargos.activado` and `reputacion.activado`, on; also in villages already generated, which are recognised by their structure and the bailiff): touching the job board lectern (or `/heraldia quest` inside the village) shows the village's board in chat, with 2 to 4 quests and clickable buttons (it works with vanilla clients). The board is the same for everyone and is rebuilt identically from the seed, the village and the period: it changes every `encargos.rotacion_dias` in-game days (3 by default); accepted quests are kept. Types: **bring** N items (food, the biome's materials or moderate ores: bread, logs, raw iron, redstone...; checked and taken when you hand it in), **defeat the guardian** of a specific unique structure within 4,000 blocks (the nearest or a farther one; any player near the guardian when it dies also gets it), **explore** a medium structure or a mine entrance within 1,500 blocks (with a hint: direction and approximate distance, never coordinates), **carry a message** to a neighbouring village along the road (if roads are enabled) and **survive wave N** of the arena (if it is placed). Several players can take the same quest; each can carry 5 at once (`/heraldia quest list`, `abandon <n>`) and hands them in at the village that gave them (`/heraldia quest turnin`). Rewards are modest: 2-12 emeralds and 20-120 experience, plus **reputation with that village** (bring +10, explore and travel +15, arena +20, guardian +30). Hitting one of its villagers costs 5 (killing it, 40) and an iron golem 3 (killing it, 20). Levels: stranger, **known** (20), **friend** (75) and **village hero** (200): its villagers lower their trades by 10, 20 and 30 % (the same calculation as Hero of the Village, which does not stack with it; the bailiff's fixed trades do not change), greet you when you trade and the board shows your title. Everything is kept per player (in their file) and the server counts what everyone has handed in (`/heraldia status`). A guardian that falls drops off the boards from the next period on; in a world that already had Heraldia, guardians defeated before this version are not recorded, so a board may ask for one of them (abandon that quest).
- **Sieges** (`aldeas.vida.asedios`, **off**: it affects balance): in fortified villages (`oscuro_fortificada`, `nieve_fortaleza`, `badlands_canon`) the weaponsmith sells at level 3 the "Siege Challenge", a level I ominous bottle (10 emeralds, once): whoever drinks it and returns to the village triggers a vanilla raid. There is no raider outpost next to the village: raiders attack villagers within 32 blocks even without seeing them, so it would have to sit outside the village, and a large village already reaches almost the 8 chunks from its start, the furthest the game places pieces; they would also be persistent mobs loaded all the time.
- **Reputation you can see** (`reputacion.aldea_visible`, on; also in villages already generated): a village shows the best reputation any player has with it, with vanilla blocks and no entity. *Known*: potted flowers of its land on the four corners of the fountain. *Friend*: banners in the village's colour on both sides of the four entrances to the square and of the job board, on the outer face of each wall gate and by each watchtower's door. *Hero*: four more pots, a lantern of the style on each side of each gate's arch, on the battlement, and a carpet in front of the job board. When everyone who has dealt with the village has negative reputation, the watchtowers close their shutters (open trapdoors over the windows). The village is shared, so it shows the *best* reputation and not each player's: a helped village is a helped village. The decorations only go on air, against the village's own blocks, and only what the mod put up is ever taken down (it records each block it places); a spot where something else turns up (a player's block, a decoration someone broke or a pot whose flower was taken) is left alone for good. It changes when a reputation crosses a level and when a village's chunks load; nothing runs per tick. With the option off, each village takes down what it put up as it loads.

Cost: one more entity per village (the bailiff, without AI) and no periodic function; trades and deeds are only evaluated when a villager levels up or a player kills, wins a raid or picks something up (vanilla advancement criteria).

## Villager conversations

The villagers of Heraldia villages talk (`dialogos.*` in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration); also in villages already generated). Everything shows [in the middle of the screen](#in-the-middle-of-the-screen), with vanilla clients too (a chest window), in each player's language; with `dialogos.en_pantalla` set to `false`, in chat with clickable buttons as before.

- **The gesture**: a right click on a **Heraldia villager standing inside a Heraldia village** first opens a conversation: a greeting and a line. The buttons below it: **[Keep talking]** (two more lines at most), **[Any news?]**, **[Trade]** (opens its trades as always) and **[Goodbye]**. **Sneak + right click goes straight to the trades**, and so does any other villager: vanilla ones, those of other mods, a Heraldia villager that has wandered out of its village, the job board's bailiff and the wizards of the huts. Holding a name tag, a lead or a spawn egg keeps their vanilla use, and babies, sleeping villagers and villagers already trading are left alone. Simulated players (Carpet bots, the fake players of trading automation) do not send the client's click, so they trade as always. With `dialogos.activado` set to `false` the whole system is off.
- **What they say** depends on who they are and where: their **profession** (each of the 15 vanilla ones, unemployed and nitwit included), the **land of the village** (its family: plains, forest, dark forest, taiga, snow, desert, savanna, jungle, swamp, cherry grove, badlands, coast), the **moment of the day** (morning, afternoon, evening, night), the **weather** at the villager (clear, rain, snow, thunderstorm; bad weather is talked about more) and **your reputation** with the village (stranger, known, friend, hero: it sets the greeting too). Each player remembers the last 24 lines they heard from any villager (in their file), so the same line does not come up twice in a row.
- **Rest**: each villager rests `dialogos.espera_segundos` (60) between one conversation and the next; meanwhile it says it is busy and offers its trades.
- **Wayfarers and their secret**: one villager in ten (`dialogos.viajeros_porcentaje`) has travelled and knows a place. Which villagers they are comes from each villager's UUID (nothing is saved; there is no telltale sign: ask around). Asked **[Any news?]** by a player with at least `dialogos.pista_reputacion` (20, *known*) reputation with the village, it tells **where a Heraldia structure lies: the direction and the distance rounded to 100 blocks, never coordinates**, like the board's explore quests. Only structures that really spawn there count: the same search as the job board (the unique structures with a guardian within 4,000 blocks, the medium structures and mine entrances within 1,500, `/locate` rules, without loading or generating chunks), one of the six nearest, chosen from the villager. **Once in its life**: it is saved in the villager (`heraldia:dialogo_aldeano`), so clicking again, another player or a restart do not get it twice; afterwards it says it has already told what it knew. Without enough reputation it hints that it knows something; with reputation disabled it tells anyone. The other villagers answer with a rumour.
- **Operators**: `/heraldia talk with <villager>` opens a conversation with that villager as a click would (to preview the lines and take screenshots).
- **The last page**: after **[Goodbye]**, or the last line of **[Keep talking]**, the villager's farewell stays on screen with only **[Close]**.

Performance: nothing runs every tick. Everything happens on a click or a button: one structure lookup to know the village (the same as the board's), a line drawn from lists already in memory (each language's file is read once) and, for a wayfarer's hint, the board's search, done once per village and server start off the server thread. What is kept: two fields in each villager that has talked and the last 24 line keys in each player's file; the conversation in course lives only in memory and is dropped when the player leaves.

### In the middle of the screen

The conversations and the arena bell's mode menu do not use the chat: they open in the middle of the screen, the way a chest does (the mouse is released, the world dims a little behind it and you choose by clicking; the server world keeps running around you, also in single player). How each player sees it is decided per player, from what their client says (`choice/Choices`):

- **With Heraldia on the client** (the same version as the server): a speech panel drawn with vanilla's own pop-up background and buttons (no texture or font of the mod's own): the speaker's name, their lines and the options stacked under them. Mouse or keyboard: the number keys pick an option, the arrow keys and Tab move between them, Enter or Space presses the one selected and Escape leaves. At most 300 wide; when it would not fit the height (GUI scale 4 on a short window) the buttons go in two columns. The texts come in your language (the same choice as the chat: `/heraldia language`, else your client's).
- **Any other client** (vanilla, or another version of Heraldia): a two-row chest window. The top row holds a sheet of paper with the speaker's name and lines (hover over it); the bottom row one stained glass pane per option, named with the option and coloured like it, its lore carrying the line (and, in the arena, the mode's help). Clicking a pane picks it. Nothing in that window can be taken, moved, shift-clicked, swapped with the number keys or the offhand, cloned, dropped or dragged, and closing it leaves your inventory exactly as it was: the window never runs the game's own click logic, and its items exist only in it.
- **In the chat, as before**: with `dialogos.en_pantalla` set to `false` (for everyone), for the fake players of automation (which cannot see a window) and as the last resort if a window cannot open (it should not happen). `/heraldia talk more|rumor|trade|bye` still work and always answer in chat (typing one closes the window).

How the server tells them apart: the mod registers its network channels on the client (`heraldia:choice_v1`, `heraldia:choice_close_v1`, `heraldia:choice_pick_v1`; the number is the protocol, raised whenever their content changes), and Fabric tells the server which channels each client registered. A client with another protocol lacks the channel and gets the chest window: nothing of another shape is ever sent or read. **The server trusts nothing the client replies**: a pick counts only for the window that is open (each new page has a new number), only for an option shown on it, once, and while it still holds (the villager alive and within 8 blocks, the player in the arena's ring); then the conversation or the arena checks its own rules again (the conversation still on and not expired, a mode still enabled, no match started). Anything else is refused, written to the log and changes nothing. The page of a wayfarer's hint arrives in the same window when the search ends.

Operators (and the server tests) have `/heraldia choice` (the open window of whoever runs it), `/heraldia choice click <slot> [<kind> [<button>]]` (a click in that window exactly as a client sends it: a real container click through the player's own connection), `/heraldia choice reply <session> <option>` (a reply exactly as the panel sends it) and `/heraldia choice send button|slot-state|bundle|pick|recipe ...` (the other packets a client sends about its open window, and a pick-block, the same way; the Herald's House look-only test uses them).

Performance: nothing per tick; a window costs one packet to open and one per click. While the chest window is open, vanilla checks every tick that it is still valid (the distance to the villager), as it does for any container.

### How to add a line

The lines are data, not code: `tools/gen/dialogue/<language>/<group>.json`, one folder per language (`es_es`, `en_us`, `fr_fr`, `de_de`, `it_it`, `pt_br`, `ca_es`, `gl_es`) and two files in each: `professions.json` (the `profession.*` pools) and `village.json` (all the others). The generator merges each language into `heraldia/dialogos/<code>.json` inside the `.jar`.

```json
{
  "profession.farmer": {
    "1": "The wheat is growing tall this year.",
    "2": "The soil of %2$s is grateful if you treat it kindly."
  }
}
```

1. **Write it in Spanish first** (`es_es` is the reference: its pools and ids are the complete set). Add it to its pool with the next free id (`"4"` after `"3"`). Ids are what each player's memory stores: never reuse an id for another meaning; to remove a line, remove it in every language.
2. **Then add the same pool and id to every other language that has that file**, `en_us` included. A language may lack a whole file (its players then get the English line); but a file that exists must have every Spanish line of that file, no more, with the same format slots.
3. **Slots** (all optional): in every pool `%1$s` is the player's name and `%2$s` the village's name; in `hint.unique`, `hint.medium` and `hint.mine`, `%1$s` is the place ("Castle of the Plains", "roadside inn"), `%2$s` the direction ("north") and `%3$s` the approximate distance in blocks. Only `%s`-style slots: a literal percent sign is `%%`. Quote the place name with the language's inner quotes (the whole line goes inside the outer ones), and phrase hints so they read well with any name (no article glued to it).
4. Check: `python3 -m pytest tools/gen/tests/test_dialogue.py -q` (it names every missing or extra line and every slot that differs) and `./gradlew build` (the generation fails on the same errors). The same test file also fails while a language lacks a group file (`test_every_language_complete`: all 8 languages have every file, so a new group needs its file in the 8).

The pools (`dialogue.pools()` in `tools/gen/dialogue.py`; the target is about 40 lines per profession and per context, some 350-400 per language):

| Pool | When | File |
|---|---|---|
| `profession.<id>` | the villager's profession: `none`, `armorer`, `butcher`, `cartographer`, `cleric`, `farmer`, `fisherman`, `fletcher`, `leatherworker`, `librarian`, `mason`, `nitwit`, `shepherd`, `toolsmith`, `weaponsmith` (weight 40) | `professions.json` |
| `family.<family>` | the village's land: `llanuras`, `bosque`, `oscuro`, `taiga`, `nieve`, `desierto`, `sabana`, `jungla`, `pantano`, `cerezos`, `badlands`, `costa` (weight 20) | `village.json` |
| `time.<moment>` | `morning`, `afternoon`, `evening`, `night` (weight 10) | `village.json` |
| `weather.<weather>` | `clear` (weight 5), `rain`, `snow`, `thunder` (weight 15) | `village.json` |
| `reputation.<tier>` | the player's level with the village: `desconocido`, `conocido`, `amigo`, `heroe` (weight 8) | `village.json` |
| `general` | anything, anywhere (weight 12) | `village.json` |
| `greeting.<tier>` | the first line of every conversation, by reputation level | `village.json` |
| `rumor` | [Any news?] to a villager that is not a wayfarer | `village.json` |
| `hint.unique`, `hint.medium`, `hint.mine` | a wayfarer tells its place (slots: place, direction, distance) | `village.json` |
| `hint.withheld` | a wayfarer that does not trust the player yet | `village.json` |
| `hint.told` | a wayfarer that has already told its place | `village.json` |
| `hint.nothing` | a wayfarer with no structure around its village | `village.json` |
| `busy` | a click while the villager rests | `village.json` |
| `farewell` | [Goodbye], or the end of the conversation | `village.json` |

A body line is drawn among the six context pools by those weights (a pool with no line, or with every line heard lately, does not count); a pool may be left empty, except `general`, `rumor`, `busy`, `farewell`, the four `greeting.*` and the six `hint.*`, which need at least one line.

## Roads between villages

Nearby Heraldia villages are joined by trails (`aldeas.caminos.*` in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration)):

- **Which villages**: each village with its nearest neighbours closer than `aldeas.caminos.distancia_maxima_bloques` (2000 by default); two villages are joined if no third one is closer to both than they are to each other, so there are no triangles and no roads crossing each other. With the default spacing (1500) almost every village has two or three roads.
- **Route**: it winds gently between the two squares, follows the terrain height and, when it fits, passes about 110 blocks from a unique structure lying halfway (with the grid in use, also the old one of `unicas.rejilla_compatible`). It stops at the pieces of a village or of any surface structure, without crossing them.
- **Surface by terrain**: dirt path (with some coarse dirt and gravel) on grass and dirt, sandstone slabs in deserts and beaches, red sandstone in badlands, gravel and cobblestone on stone and snow, packed mud in mangroves. Width `aldeas.caminos.ancho` (3 by default), with ragged edges.
- **Water**: a river or a short stretch of water (up to about 64 blocks) is crossed by a plank bridge one block above the water, with posts down to the bottom; a shallow marsh (2 blocks), by a boardwalk flush with the water; long and deep water (lakes, sea) cuts the road at the shore. It never goes over lava.
- **Lamp posts and signs**: every 48 blocks or so, on alternating sides, a fence post with a lantern or a torch (depending on the road), in the biome's wood; about 128 blocks from each village, a sign with the name of the destination village (a place name such as "Torre del Roble" or "Vado de la Encina", the same on all its signs) and the blocks left, with an arrow towards it.

Everything comes from the seed and the village grid, chunk by chunk, without loading or waiting for any other chunk: both sides of a chunk border always match. **In an existing world, new chunks only**: what is already generated does not change and the stretches that would fall in explored land do not exist (the road starts at the border). Cost, measured on the test server (`tools/smoke/roads.py`, about 1,000 chunks): half of the chunks pay less than 32 µs and 90 % less than 128 µs; the only expensive parts are computed once per server start and kept in memory: whether a grid cell has a village (about 1.5 ms; at most 100 cells with a village), each road's waypoint (about 30 ms) and the bottom height at the vertices that fall in water (about 0.4 ms each). The mod writes these figures to the log when the server stops.

## Caravans

Caravans sometimes travel the [roads](#roads-between-villages) (`caravanas.*` in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration); also in an existing world, but only along roads that really exist):

- **When**: every minute, if there are players in the overworld and no caravan on the road (`caravanas.max_activas`, 1 by default), one is drawn with chance `caravanas.frecuencia` (10 %) next to one of them, at least 2 minutes after the previous one. It sets off from a point of the road 24 to 80 blocks from the player, in a chunk already loaded (it never loads or generates chunks) and only if the road is really there in the world: in chunks generated before the roads the route exists but the road does not, and the caravan does not set off (or ends where the road ends). Operators send one whenever they want with `/heraldia caravan spawn`.
- **Who**: a **Heraldia Merchant** (a wandering trader with its trades, 40 health) with 1 to 3 pack llamas and, one time in four, an iron golem as a guard. It travels about 128 blocks of the road (8 waypoints of the canonical route between two neighbouring villages, away from the squares) towards the destination village, passing in front of the player; the llamas and the guard follow it.
- **Ambush** (`caravanas.prob_asalto`, 35 %; 15 % with the casual profile and 60 % with hardcore): halfway, 2 to 4 **bandits** (one more than the players nearby) come out on both sides of the road, those of the [bandit camp](#medium-structures): pillagers and, with 3 or more, their vindicator chief, with the rules of anchored mobs and Heraldia's [difficulty](#restocking-loot-and-difficulty). The caravan stops (the merchant runs from them) until none is left.
- **Reward**: on arrival, each player within 48 blocks of the merchant gets 2 emeralds plus one per 4 waypoints (4 with the whole stretch) and +5 [reputation](#village-life) with the destination village; if they defended it from an ambush, 4 more emeralds, a modest item (name tag, spyglass, leads, golden carrots, honey, map, bundle or saddle), +15 reputation and the **Safe Passage** advancement.
- **Loss**: if the merchant dies (at anyone's hands), the caravan is lost: no reward, and neither it, nor the llamas, nor the guard drop anything.
- **Farewell**: if no player is within 64 blocks for 30 s, if its chunks unload, if the road ends, if it gets stuck or after 10 minutes (3 under attack), it takes its leave and all its entities are removed (the bandits too).

Performance: no per-tick work except the caravan on the road (one step per second with a few entities) and the draw every minute; the route is looked up off the server thread with the road layout, which is already in memory. Nothing is saved: when the server stops its entities are removed and, at startup, any caravan entity that loads (from a chunk unloaded mid-journey) is removed, so there are never orphaned or duplicated ones.

## Wayfarers

Besides the caravans, people pass along the [roads](#roads-between-villages) near the players: life you come across without looking for it (`viajeros.*` in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration), each kind with its own switch; also in an existing world, only along roads that really exist). They use the caravans' engine: the same search for a stretch of road 24 to 80 blocks from the player in chunks already loaded, the same journey of up to 8 waypoints (about 128 blocks, away from the squares) and the same goodbye and cleanup.

- **When**: every minute (half a minute after the caravans' draw), with chance `viajeros.frecuencia` (30 %) next to a player, at least 2 minutes after the previous one and with at most `viajeros.max_activos` (1) on the road on the whole server, apart from the caravans; never within 160 blocks of another caravan or wayfarer. People travel by day; by night only the guards patrol. Operators bring one whenever they want with `/heraldia caravan wayfarer [pilgrim | shepherd | messenger | patrol | hunter]` (without a kind, the draw chooses one that fits the area; with a kind, it ignores the area and the hour), and `/heraldia caravan list` and `clear` include them. When one sets off, the players within 64 blocks read in the action bar who passes and where it goes.
- **Who**, by the area's village family (the biome tags of the village styles):
  - **Pilgrim** (1 entity): walks alone, with its staff, towards the nearest **monastery** or **cathedral** that really spawns within 3,000 blocks (the same check as `/locate`); if there is none, there is no pilgrim, and it walks the stretch in the direction that takes it closer. It does not trade.
  - **Shepherd** (4 to 6): drives 3 to 5 sheep towards the neighbouring village, mostly white and the rest in the colors of the land (grey and brown in the taiga, black in the dark forest, pink in the cherry groves...); not in desert, badlands, jungle or swamp. Sells 2 wool of its flock's colors (1 emerald, 4 times per color), 4 cooked mutton (1 emerald, twice) and buys 18 wheat for 1 emerald (3 times).
  - **Messenger** (1): runs twice as fast as a merchant with a letter for the neighbouring village and does not stop: it does not trade.
  - **Pair of guards** (2): two iron golems of the village of origin ("Guard of ...") patrol towards the next one, by day and by night; they fight the monsters they meet and defend each other.
  - **Hunter** (2): comes back to its village with the area's game in its hands (rabbit in open land, boar in the taiga and the dark forest, fowl in jungle and swamp) and its dog, a tame wolf of the local variant that defends it. Sells 3 cooked game (1 emerald), hides, leather or feathers (1 emerald), 8 arrows (1 emerald) and, where there are rabbits, a rabbit's foot (3 emeralds, once).
- **Trades**: cheap and few (a wayfarer passes once and does not restock), with nothing that breaks the progression (no enchantments, netherite or elytra). There is no reward for meeting them: they are life on the road.
- **Attacking them**: hurting one costs reputation with its village of origin, as hurting a villager or a golem of the village does (−5 for a person, −3 for a guard, −2 for an animal; once per player and encounter), and killing it costs more (−40, −20, −10); the player is told that word will reach that village. The guards answer together and the hunter's dog defends its owner. Killing them drops nothing (no farming).
- **Farewell**: as the caravans, if no player is within 64 blocks for 30 s, if its chunks unload, if the road ends, if it gets stuck or after 10 minutes. At the end of its stretch, if a player is within 32 blocks, it does not vanish in sight: it walks on towards its village and is removed when nobody is near (1 minute at most). If its leader dies, the rest are removed.

Performance: the same as a caravan, and lighter. One encounter at a time (configurable up to 3), from 1 to 6 vanilla entities with simple goal AI (wandering traders, iron golems, sheep and a wolf: no villager brains, beds or job sites), on the road for about a minute (the messenger, half; the slowest, the guards, about two) and 10 minutes at most; one step per second for the one on the road and a draw per minute; the stretch and the shrine are looked up off the server thread (the shrine check is done once per candidate cell and remembered). Nothing is saved: their entities carry the caravans' tag and are removed when the server stops or if they load without their encounter.

## Village events

Things happen in the villages (`eventos.*` in the [configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration); also in villages already generated). Now and then, while you are in a Heraldia village, its square holds an event for a few minutes; then everything goes, and the square is left exactly as it was.

- **When**: every 30 s, for each village a player is in (more than 24 blocks from its square, so it is not laid out in front of them), an event starts with chance `eventos.frecuencia` (20 %) if the village has rested `eventos.espera_minutos` (20 minutes of play) since its last one and fewer than `eventos.max_activos` (2) are running on the server. One per village, never two. The kind fits the hour and the weather and is not the same as the village's last one. Everyone within 160 blocks is told it has begun.
- **Market day** (in the morning, rain or shine; 6 minutes): two to four stalls with striped red and white awnings along the avenues' entrances, each with its goods on the counter (a hay bale, a melon, a bale of wool, dried kelp) and a **trader** behind it with real trades of the village's produce (bread, pumpkin pie and cake; fruit; wool, carpets and a bed; cooked fish), modest and in emeralds, with your reputation's discount; shoppers with emeralds in their hands in front. The traders call out their goods now and then.
- **Wedding** (by day, fine weather only; 4 minutes): a white and pink arch at the head of a carpet aisle between two lanterns; the **bride** (with a bouquet), the **groom** and the **cleric** under it, the guests in two rows facing them, flowers in hand, and two children at the back. The bell rings and a firework goes up when it starts; the cleric's three lines of the ceremony follow and, at the "yes", the bell, three fireworks and hearts.
- **Funeral** (by day, rain or shine; 3 minutes): a bier of dark wood under a black pall between two soul lanterns; the **cleric** at its head, the **widow** beside it with a wither rose, and the mourners around it, some with candles. The bell tolls slowly the whole time; the cleric says the eulogy in three parts.
- **Festival** (at dusk, fine weather; 5 minutes): a bonfire, two maypoles, a **band** of three playing a folk tune (note-block flute and bass, heard within 64 blocks, with notes over their heads) and a ring of dancers who hop and turn to it; the **festival host** sells cookies, cake, honey and fireworks. It ends with three fireworks.
- **Seen from afar**: the main figures carry their names over their heads ("The bride", "Cleric", "Market trader"...: name tags show up to 64 blocks), the props stand two to four blocks high, and the bell, the band and the fireworks are heard and seen from further.
- **Taking part** (all of it with vanilla clients, in each player's language, through the [villagers' lines](#villager-conversations)): come close and the main figure greets you; **buy** at the market or from the festival's host; give the couple a **gift** (a flower, an emerald, a cake, gold, a diamond, honey, cookies) or the widow a flower, a candle or an emerald, and they thank you; **ask the band for a song** (they change the tune); right-click a guest and they tell you something. Each event gives **reputation** with the village for taking part, once per player: +2 for a gift, +1 for a purchase or a song. Hitting one of its figures spoils it: everyone scatters, and it costs the reputation of hitting a villager.
- **The square stays as it was**: an event only puts blocks on **air**, on a solid floor, in the free cells of one bay of the square (two for the market) and never where the job board is, on the [reputation decorations'](#village-life) spots, under a block or where something stands; if a player has built on the square, or the square is missing (a village that lost it on rough terrain), the event is smaller, goes to another bay or does not happen. Everything it puts up is recorded first (`heraldia:eventos_aldea`, in the world), and at the end only what is **still exactly what it put up**, with nothing resting on it, is taken down; survival players cannot break the props while it runs. The figures are removed when it ends, when the server stops, and if any of them loads later (a crash), they are removed then; recorded blocks of an unloaded chunk are taken down when it loads.
- **Operators**: `/heraldia event start <market | wedding | funeral | festival>` starts one in the village you are in (ignoring the hour, the weather, the frequency and the rest, never the limits or the room), `/heraldia event stop [all]` ends it (or all), `/heraldia event list` shows the running ones and `/heraldia event with <entity>` deals with a figure as your click would.

Performance: the figures are vanilla villagers **without AI** (no brain, goals, pathfinding or movement: they stand, turn towards you and speak; they claim no bed or job site and cannot be hurt, converted or killed by mobs): 10 to 15 per event, at most 18 entities with the fireworks (the cap is 25). With no event running, nothing runs but a modulo per tick and a structure lookup per player every 30 s; a running event is updated once a second, and the festival's tune every 4 ticks only while it plays. Measured cost: see [docs/DEVELOPMENT.md](DEVELOPMENT.md), "Play cost". With the [light profile](CONFIGURATION.md#light-profile) there are no events.

## Atlantis

Exactly one per world, always, with no configuration option. It is a walled city of about 770 × 770 blocks, fully submerged, in the deep-ocean basin closest to the origin that is 1,500 blocks or more away and unexplored (in an existing world it only appears in new chunks). The position is computed the first time the world loads and saved in `data/heraldia/atlantida.json`.

- Find it with `/locate structure heraldia:unicas/atlantida` or with its treasure map.
- Flooded streets and squares, and more than 200 houses, towers and rooms that are **dry**, entered through doors or by diving up from below. If a wall is broken, the room floods.
- Eight districts (agora, fish market, baths, arsenal, sunken harbor, coral gardens, academy and amphitheater), a temple in the center, underground galleries and, at the bottom, the final guardian.
- Defenders: guardians, drowned with tridents, 48 spawners, elder guardians in the key districts and the Guardian of Poseidon.
- Three seals with their keys, combination doors, arrow traps, water and TNT.
- 185 chests with advanced loot and a unique chest, hidden and locked with the final guardian's key, holding two legendary tridents. Its contents are fixed: the first group to arrive takes them, unless `unicas.atlantida.cofre_unico_por_jugador` is enabled (see Configuration): then each player opens their own copy.
- No other structures (monuments, shipwrecks, ruins) generate inside the city, except strongholds, which the game needs to reach the End.

Details, plans and what has been verified: [docs/unicas/atlantida.md](unicas/atlantida.md).

- They never spawn inside a village, a unique structure, Atlantis or the Corsair's Isle: not within `medianas.distancia_aldeas_unicas_bloques` (256) of the start of a Heraldia village or unique structure that spawns there, nor within `medianas.distancia_minima.bloques` (192) of another medium structure of a different type (a deterministic draw keeps one, the same rule as the minimum distance between unique structures), nor within 128 blocks of a vanilla village candidate. They also give way to structures that show above ground, vanilla or from other mods (pillager outposts, temples, ruined portals, other mods' villages...): one is discarded if such a structure spawns within 80 blocks (vanilla) or 128 (other mods).
- **Inhabitants** (`medianas.habitantes`, on by default; off in the [light profile](CONFIGURATION.md#light-profile)): the innkeeper and the gardener, the miller, the fisher, the hunter and the archer, the lookout and the hermit **live** in their building, with vanilla AI: each one has its own bed and a workstation of its trade, side by side on walkable ground (no ladder on the way: villagers do not climb them), goes to work in the morning, idles around the building's **call bell** (its meeting point, which keeps it from wandering off) and sleeps at night. They keep the fixed trades and names they had and stay invulnerable (alone in the wild, the first night's zombies would empty the building). At most 2 per structure and never a free bed among them, so they do not breed and never summon a golem: the number stays fixed. The bandit camp, the ruined shrine, the watchtower with the archer, the wizard's hut and the Nether and End structures keep the occupants they had (hostile or their own); nothing of Heraldia runs every tick for them. They do not have the [village conversations](#villager-conversations): those need a Heraldia village around the villager (its land, style and reputation), and a medium structure has none. **In an existing world** they only appear in medium structures generated from now on; those already generated keep their still figure. Turning the option off only affects inhabitants that have never loaded yet (the new ones do not appear and the old figures stay still, as before); one that has already loaded once is never touched again.
- Find them with `/locate structure #heraldia:medianas/<type>` (or `heraldia:medianas/<type>/<style>`) and `/heraldia info nearby`; `/heraldia info` shows their configuration. To test one: `/place structure heraldia:medianas/<type>/<style>`.
- Frequency, measured with `MediumFrequencyTest` (the placement grids on the real 26.3 Overworld biome noise, with villages, unique structures and vanilla structures around them, 3 seeds, 16,000 × 16,000 blocks): see the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md) and the pull request for the figures per type, and [Medium structures density](CONFIGURATION.md#medium-structures-density) for the figures at each frequency (`MediumDensityComparisonTest`). Each grid looks for its candidate inside the type's biomes (the spot of the cell most inside them), so a type appears wherever its biomes are, at most once per cell.
- Configuration: `medianas.*` options (see [Configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration)). Advancements: **By the Wayside**, **Wayfarer** and one of its own for each type of batch 2 (see [Advancements](#advancements)). **In an existing world they only appear in new chunks.**

## Nether and End

Heraldia also builds in the other two dimensions (T44). Everything below appears **only in new chunks** of the Nether
and the End, never touches the End's central island or the dragon fight, and follows the same rules as in the Overworld
(minimum distance between medium structures and between unique structures, giving way to vanilla structures such as
bastions, fortresses and End cities, and to other mods' structures), counting only the ones of the same dimension.

- **Height.** They use their own structure type, `heraldia:dimension`: in the Nether the surface heightmap is the
  bedrock roof, so the start height is taken from the noise column (from y = 112 down to y = 34, above the lava sea),
  the first solid floor with air for the whole template above it, with the four corners of the footprint within ±5
  blocks (up to 4 stacked cave floors and 5 points of the candidate chunk are tried). In the End the four corners and
  the centre must stand on an island (height ≥ 45, never over the void) and the site must be more than 1,024 blocks
  from the centre of the End. `/place structure` places them anywhere (at y = 64 if there is no floor).
- **Placement grids**: random candidate in each cell (the biome search of the Overworld grids only reads Overworld
  noise) and no exclusion zone for vanilla villages. Options as usual: `medianas.<type>.*` and `unicas.<id>.*`.

## Wizard's huts

One more kind of [medium structure](#medium-structures) (`heraldia:medianas/choza_mago/<style>`, T43), with the same placement rules, options (`medianas.choza_mago.activada`, `medianas.choza_mago.separacion_bloques`: 1024) and lookup (`/locate structure #heraldia:medianas/choza_mago`). About one every ~2,000 blocks (`MediumFrequencyTest`).

- **The hut**: a 17 × 17 yard with a 9 × 9 hut under a steep roof. Ground floor: bookshelves, a lectern with *The Wizard's Notes* (translatable written book), the study chest, a counter with two brewing stands, an amethyst cluster and candles, a full and an empty cauldron, a decorated pot, a rug, two lanterns and the fireplace (a campfire in a niche of the south wall and a hollow flue up past the ridge). Attic up a ladder: bed, the attic chest, cobwebs in the corners, amethyst, candles and the **star map** (two banners with globe, star and sun patterns on the gables). Variant a: a fenced herb and mushroom garden (two raised beds, a composter, a lantern) and Shadow, the wizard's black cat; b: a stone circle with candles around an amethyst cluster and four mushrooms, and two herb beds in front of the door.
- **Styles**: dark oak (dark forest and pale garden), mangrove **on stilts** (swamps and mangrove swamps; the hut 3 blocks above the ground, with steps up), spruce (taigas), stone (windswept hills and stony peaks), cherry (cherry groves) and sandstone (desert); each with its plants, its typical potion and its book.
- **The wizard**: a still cleric villager (no AI, invulnerable, persistent, tag `heraldia_mago`) with a generated name ("Morwen of the Mist": 16 names and 8 translated epithets) and fixed trades: 4 glass bottles, 6 lapis lazuli, three potions (healing and two of the style), a modest enchanted book (level II, with a book) and an ender pearl for emeralds, and he buys nether wart. Advancement **Sorcerer's Apprentice** for trading with one.
- **The evil wizard**: from the world seed and the wizard's chunk (SplitMix64), `chozas_mago.prob_malvado` % of the huts (20; 10 with casual and 35 with hardcore) have an evil wizard; the same hut always gives the same, and the decision is stored in the wizard's tags when it loads (changing the option later does not change those already seen). He reveals himself **only once**, when a player (not a spectator) comes within 7 blocks or tries to trade: a translated chat message, particles and evoker sounds, and the villager becomes the **Evil Wizard**, an evoker with 48 health, the phases, reinforcements and boss bar of the [guardians](#guardian-fights) and the [difficulty](#restocking-loot-and-difficulty), who comes with 3 vexes ("flying devils") and then summons his own like any evoker. When he dies, his vexes vanish.
- **Loot** (only if a player defeats him): `heraldia:chozas_mago/malvado`, a level above a medium structure (emeralds, lapis, bottles o' enchanting, ender pearls, glowstone dust, amethyst, gold; and one of: an enchanted book of level 15-25, a strength or regeneration potion, long night vision, diamonds, a golden apple or an enchanted iron sword). With `chozas_mago.prob_botin_premium` % (30, also per hut, when he reveals himself) he also drops **a prize** that does not break progression: a Mending book, Turtle Master potions (normal or strong), long Slow Falling, Strength II or Regeneration II, a **totem of undying** or a book of Protection IV, Efficiency V, Sharpness V, Unbreaking III, Fortune III or Looting III. No netherite, elytra or enchanted apples (generation fails otherwise). **He does not drop the vanilla evoker's totem**: his table replaces the evoker's, because huts are common and a guaranteed totem would turn each one into a farm; it stays as one of the prize options. Advancement **No Tricks Left** for defeating one.
- **Quests and treasure**: the hut is just another medium structure, so the job board's explore quests can send you to one (a visit to the wizard), and its chests carry the [treasure hunt](#treasure-hunt) clue and restock like any medium structure's. There is no "defeat the evil wizard" quest: whether a wizard is evil is only known when his hut loads, and the job board does not load chunks.
- **Commands** (operators): `/heraldia wizard` lists the wizards within 32 blocks and their state; `/heraldia wizard reveal` reveals the nearest one even if he is benign (for testing). `/heraldia status` shows both chances and how many Evil Wizards have fallen since startup.
- Performance: one bounded entity query (a 7-block box) per player per second and an entity-load event with an early exit on tags. No mixins and nothing saved apart. **In an existing world, only in new chunks.**

Each one leads down **for real** to a Heraldia gallery of its own: 3-wide tunnels with timber frames and lanterns, a rail track from the yard to the end of the main line, crossings, loops and rooms. | **Mining camp** | `heraldia:minas/mediana/<style>/<variant>` | the mouth, the foreman's cabin (bed, table, chest), an ore heap, a loaded chest minecart on its rails and a small smelter (blast furnace, furnace, anvil, a chimney with a smoking campfire) | a 16-block descent to a gallery of 5 × 3 crossings with store room, ore room, canteen and the spiders' room with a small cave spider spawner |

- **Styles by biome**, two variants each (the yard mirrored and another gallery): oak and stone on windswept hills, meadows and cherry groves; spruce in taigas, groves and snowy slopes; sandstone (and birch) in the desert; terracotta (and dark oak) in the badlands; deepslate (and spruce) on the peaks. - **Placement**: one grid per size (`heraldia:minas/<size>`, by default one every 768, 1,152 and 1,920 blocks among those of the same size, scaled by `minas.entradas.separacion_bloques`), looking for its candidate inside the styles' biomes. Two kinds of **coastal** structure, always on a real shoreline: the custom type `heraldia:costa` looks for the shore around the candidate (low land with water next to it, on an 8-block grid up to 32 blocks around, refined to the last block of land) and turns the template to face the sea.

## Ambience

Effects with the game's own sounds and particles: nothing new to download, vanilla clients hear them as they are, and
the mod still registers no block, item or entity of its own. Each sound is sent **only to the players it is meant for**,
in the **Ambient/Environment** sound category (each player turns it down with their own slider), and each player has a
cooldown per effect, so it never repeats every little while. They work in existing worlds too (they look for the
structures already generated). Options: `ambiente.activado` (all of them) and one per effect.

- **Village bell** (`ambiente.campanas`): at **sunrise** and at **nightfall** (world clock 0 and 12,000) three bell strikes
  ring out from the village square for the players inside a Heraldia village (its box: streets, houses and outskirts).
  Waking up after sleeping puts the clock on a sunrise, so it greets the morning. At most twice a day per player (5 min of
  cooldown, so `/time set` does not make it ring again and again); with the daylight cycle stopped it rings once.
- **Lighthouse siren** (`ambiente.sirena_faro`): in **rain or a thunderstorm** (vanilla has no fog weather; rain is its
  low visibility), the nearest lighthouse (a coastal one or the smugglers' lighthouse) sounds a deep horn (the "Ponder"
  goat horn, low) for the players **out in the open** within **160 blocks**: from a boat at sea it is heard far off and
  faint. Not in caves or under water. At most once every **3 minutes** per player.
- **Mine galleries** (`ambiente.minas`): underground in a Heraldia mine (the three sizes of mine entrance and the Lost
  Mine of Hondavena), every **20-45 s** a drop of water hangs from the ceiling near the player and falls (the game's
  dripstone drop, which plays its own drip when it lands) and, now and then, the **cave's echo** sounds a dozen blocks down
  the gallery (at most every 4 min). Nothing under the open sky.
- **Cost**: nothing runs on the server tick except `tick % 100`; every 5 s a loop over the players; per player, one structure lookup in
  their own chunk every 20-45 s (mines) and one more only in the minutes of sunrise and nightfall (villages) and, only while it rains and
  the player's siren is due, a look at the structure starts of the loaded chunks around them (it never loads a chunk).
  Nothing is saved in the world.
- **Not included: the tavern song on the Corsair's Isle.** The Hanged Mermaid has no villagers inside (its template only
  has the corsair's crew in the caves) and the game has no song: villager murmurs coming out of an empty tavern would be a
  ghost choir, and a tune of note-block sounds played every few minutes gets tiresome. It would need villagers in the
  template (only in new chunks), so it is left out on purpose.

## Treasure maps

Every unique structure has its own treasure map, which marks the nearest one with an X (or another mark specific to the structure) and carries its translated name:

- In village epic chests: 5 % of the chests hold one (for a random unique structure).
- For sale from master cartographers (level 5) **in Heraldia villages** (not vanilla ones): 24 emeralds and a compass, 2 per restock. Villagers in villages generated before this version do not offer it.
- The search covers about 10,000 blocks and does not load already explored chunks (so it does not stall the server). If no structure of that type is within reach, no map appears (the chest leaves it empty and the cartographer does not offer that trade).
- To test: `/loot give @s loot heraldia:mapas/<id>` gives the map for structure `heraldia:unicas/<id>` from where you stand.

On slopes, building foundations (and the piles of the stilted styles) reach down to the ground in the style's material, up to 16 blocks.

Known limitations: on slopes the streets follow the terrain with one-block steps and, on a cliff higher than 16 blocks, a foundation may be left hanging; a coastal village may still have no harbor when every shore within about 96 blocks is taken by the square, special buildings or streets reaching the sea (with the test seeds, 21 of 21 natural coastal villages got one); overlap with structures from other mods has not been checked (biomes from other mods have, see [Biomes from other mods](COMPATIBILITY.md#biomes-from-other-mods)).

## Treasure hunt

A chained hunt through real Heraldia structures to a buried chest (Java package `tesoro/` and `tools/gen/treasure.py`). Options `tesoro.*` (see [Configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#treasure-hunt-heraldia-treasure)); they reload with `/heraldia reload` and none of them touches world generation.

- **The first clue**: a *treasure map fragment* (paper with a name and the `custom_data` mark `heraldia_busqueda`). It turns up with probability `tesoro.probabilidad` % (3 by default) in every chest, barrel or pot of medium structures and mine entrances, half that in unique structures' ones (never in trap dispensers) the same in the pay for each job-board quest handed in (table `heraldia:tesoro/encargo`, rolled by `Treasure.reward` in `Quests.pay`) and five times that in the job-board feats' reward. The condition (`heraldia:tesoro_pista`) reads the option when the loot is rolled. To hand one out: `/heraldia treasure give` (operators) or the `heraldia:tesoro/fragmento` table.
- **Deciphering it**: right-click. The chain is chosen **off the server thread** with the same rules as `/heraldia info nearby`: a medium structure within `tesoro.radio_max` blocks (1500 by default) of where it is deciphered; from it, a mine entrance (or, if there is none, another medium structure); and from the last one, the treasure, 48-128 blocks away. Structures already generated are preferred (read from disk; loaded chunks are confirmed on the server thread); otherwise predicted ones, in ungenerated chunks where generation will place them (the map says so: "in lands not yet explored"). **It never loads or generates a chunk.** It is deterministic: the seed comes from the world, the player and how many clues they have deciphered; same seed, same chain. On the test server it takes ~0.4-0.5 s on another thread (about 35 grid cells).
- **The map**: the fragment becomes a vanilla map (`filled_map`, visible on any client) with the current destination's mark, its number ("Treasure map (1/3)") and the clue in the lore: what it is (a stop along the road, a mine mouth, the earth where Ysolda dug), the biome and the direction from the previous mark. The whole chain (seed, destinations, step) is stored in the item itself: it survives restarts and the player's death like any item.
- **Each mark**: used far away, the map tells how many blocks away and in which direction the mark is; within 40 blocks of a structure, it turns into the map of the next mark.
- **The treasure**: within 32 blocks of the final mark, the chest is buried (one block under the surface) at the safe spot closest to the mark: natural ground, no water above, outside the pieces of any structure and **with nothing built by players** (planks, glass, wool, doors, torches, chests, rails, bricks...) within 6 blocks around, from 6 blocks below to 10 above the ground. Only chunks already loaded are looked at. If nothing fits (someone built there, or it is water), the mark moves 32-64 blocks and the map is updated. Finding the spot costs ~12-14 ms once, on the server thread.
- **The chest** (table `heraldia:tesoro/cofre`, "Ysolda's chest"): *Ysolda's Compass* (a named keepsake) and 3-5 valuable items (diamonds, emeralds, gold, an enchanted book, a heart of the sea, the wayfinder armour trim...) that do not break progression (no netherite, elytra or enchanted apples), plus provisions. Each chain is completed **only once**: its seed is stored in the world, so a copy of the map (cartography table) gives no second chest.
- **Advancements**: `tesoro/fragmento` (get a fragment) and `tesoro/cofre` (open the buried chest). The Herald's Guide has its page ("Treasure hunt").
- Server test: `tools/smoke/treasure.py` (loot and reload, the whole chain with restarts halfway and at the end, the chest outside a house placed on the mark, its loot and its advancements).

## Guardian fights

The named guardians of the unique structures (and the Warden of Poseidon) are bosses with **three phases**, including those of structures already generated in an existing world. It all runs on the server: clients do not need the mod.

- A vanilla **boss bar** with the guardian's name for players within 32 blocks while the fight lasts (it starts with the first hit the guardian deals or takes); yellow in phase 1, red in phase 2 and purple in phase 3, with notches at the thirds. It goes away when the guardian dies or nobody is left within 48 blocks.
- **Phase 2** below 66 % health and **phase 3** below 33 %: it becomes enraged (speed I and resistance I for 20 s and strength I for 6 s; in phase 3, speed II and resistance I for 30 s and strength II for 8 s), with smoke, soul flames and a roar, and nearby players see a notice in the action bar ("Sir Aldric, el Castellano Maldito is enraged and calls for reinforcements").
- **Reinforcements**: at each phase, only once, it calls 2 or 3 mobs that suit its structure (the castle garrison with swords and helmets, the colosseum gladiators, the corsair's crew, watchers and a trident-wielding sentinel in Atlantis...; the rest, by their guardian: husks with the centurion, strays with the Frost King, illagers with the illagers...). There are never more than 4 alive at once near the guardian; they drop neither its loot nor its trophy (nor the gear they carry), always spawn as adults and stay put until the fight, like the template mobs, and despawn like any monster if everyone leaves. No reinforcements on peaceful.
- It only changes phase with a player within 48 blocks (if it gets hurt with nobody around, by the sun or lava, the phase comes with the next hit).
- Each phase is recorded in the guardian's own tags (`heraldia_fase.2`, `heraldia_fase.3`): it does not repeat even if the guardian heals, the chunk unloads or the server restarts. Reinforcements carry the `heraldia_refuerzo` tag.
- They can be turned off separately with `jefes.fases.activado`, `jefes.refuerzos.activado` and `jefes.barra.activado` (see [Configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration)).

### Cleared uniques

When the guardian of a unique structure falls, **the spawners of that structure turn off for good**: the place is left clean and safe to live in, as the reward for the fight, and it stops being an endless experience farm (the same reasoning as the [arena](#wave-arena), which gives 15 % experience and no items). Option `jefes.apagar_generadores` (on by default; `false` keeps them working as before).

- **What "that structure" is**: the whole structure the guardian belongs to (the saved start of `heraldia:unicas/<id>` that reaches the guardian's chunk), with all its pieces: also the dungeons and towers far from the guardian's room. Not a radius around the guardian: a vanilla dungeon, a mineshaft or a player's spawner next to it is never touched.
- **Which spawners**: those its templates placed, found from the templates themselves at their position in the world (so they are known without loading their chunks), and only if a spawner is still there (one already broken is skipped). There are 1 to 4 in each unique and 48 spread over the tiles of Atlantis (107 with one of each kind).
- **How they turn off**: the cage stays, as a relic of the fight, but empty (no mob to spawn): it spawns nothing again. Like any vanilla empty spawner, a spawn egg can fill it again. The mobs already alive in the structure stay.
- **Cost**: nothing runs per tick. It hangs from the guardian's death (an event the mod already listens to) and takes a few milliseconds once. Spawners in chunks still in memory but no longer active are turned off at once too; those in chunks that are not in memory are written down in the world (attachment `heraldia:unicas_vencidas`, per dimension) and turned off when their chunk loads; while nothing is pending, the chunk-load check is one empty lookup.
- **Idempotent**: each cleared structure is recorded once; another guardian dying in it (one brought back with `/place`, another mod) does nothing more. With the option off when the guardian falls, the structure is still recorded as cleared (for its premium chest, below) but no spawner is touched; with it turned off afterwards, pending spawners wait until it is on again.
- **Structures placed with `/place structure`** do not save their start (see `docs/LESSONS.md`): their spawners are not turned off (the log says so).
- **In an existing world**: a unique whose guardian died **before this version** is not recorded as cleared and **stays as it was** (its spawners keep working). Detecting it on its own ("the guardian is not there") was ruled out: in 26.3 entities load separately from their chunk, and a guardian that chased a player out of its structure and died there, or whose entities are not loaded yet, would read as dead. A wrong guess would turn off the spawners of a structure nobody has beaten, and that cannot be undone. The downside: those already beaten uniques keep their farms until a player breaks the spawners with a pickaxe (vanilla, as always). Every unique beaten from this version on turns off on its own.
- **Restocking loot** ([`botin.reponer.activado`](#restocking-loot-and-difficulty), off by default): a cleared unique still restocks like villages and medium structures, which have no fight either, **except its premium chest**, which from then on only gets a modest refill (see [Premium chest of a cleared unique](#premium-chest-of-a-cleared-unique)).
- Log: `Heraldia: bosses: <guardian> defeated: <structure> cleared, N spawners turned off, M pending until their chunk loads (… ms): <positions>`; and later `Heraldia: bosses: N pending spawners turned off when chunk … loaded`.
- Tests: `ClearedSpawnersTest` (JUnit: the record, idempotency, pending per chunk, saved format, a real spawner turned off) and `tools/smoke/cleared_spawners.py` (server, ~90 s: a really generated castle, its guardian killed after a restart with only its chunk loaded, the loaded spawners off at once and the other one when its chunk loads, nothing more on a second death, also after a restart).

## End of the Chronicle

The Chronicle used to have no end: Atlantis's leaf points back to the first, and beating the last guardian closed nothing. Now it does. When **every guardian of the main world's unique structures** has fallen to a player, the last oath is broken and Ysolda's last word reaches them:

- **The title, Herald of Atlantis**: a challenge advancement (`heraldia:cronica/final`, 500 experience), announced in chat like any challenge, shown on the player's screen (title and subtitle) and under their name in `/heraldia journal`.
- **The last leaf**: a written book by Ysolda ("Chronicle: the last leaf", epic) that closes the story: she never asked for her guardians to be defeated, only to wait for someone worthy; the way back was never in the leaves but in whoever followed them; and she hands over the standard of the order. In the player's language (also with a vanilla client: the text the client cannot translate comes in its language, as in the Herald's Guide).
- **The Standard of the Heralds**: a blue banner with the sea rising, the waves and the Order's golden seal and border, epic, that **can be placed** anywhere. Its name, pattern and rarity stay when it is placed and picked up again (the vanilla banner keeps those); the lore with who raised it, only on the item given. It is given once, like the advancement.
- **Beyond the Map** (optional): the Nether and End guardians do not count towards the end. A player who also defeats them gets a second challenge (`heraldia:cronica/mas_alla`, 250) and their last leaf is swapped for one with one more page; until then, that page tells them two leaves were carried beyond the world, without asking them to go.

**Which guardians.** One per unique structure that can still appear in this world (enabled in the configuration) or whose guardian the player already defeated (even if it was disabled later), as the journal counts them: today 20 in the main world (Atlantis included) and 2 beyond.

**When a guardian counts as defeated by a player** (all three are facts the game recorded, none is a guess):
- they have its guardian advancement (`unicas/<id>/guardian`, earned by the killing blow), **also from before this version**;
- they **took part** in its fall: they were within 64 blocks (the quests' participation radius) when a guardian that a player had hurt died. In a group, everyone in the fight counts, not only whoever landed the blow;
- it exists **once per world** (Atlantis) and has already fallen to anyone: nobody else could ever beat it, so it counts for every player. The world learns it from the death or, for one beaten before this version, from its killer's advancement when they join.

A guardian that dies with no player in the fight (the sun, `/kill`, a trap) is nobody's victory.

**Per player.** Like reputation, quests and the journal: on a server, whoever closes the Chronicle gets the title, the leaf and the standard; the others see the announcement and keep their own count. What is per world are the facts about places (spawners off, the modest premium chest, the once-per-world guardian fallen).

**In an existing world**: players whose guardian advancements are all there already (they beat them before this version) get the end the next time they join. Players who only fought alongside someone else before this version are not credited with those guardians (only the killer's advancement was recorded then); an operator can grant one with `/advancement grant <player> only heraldia:unicas/<id>/guardian`. Every fight from this version on is credited to everyone in it.

**Option** `guia.final_cronica` (on by default). Off, nothing is given and the journal leaves the Chronicle out; the record of who took part goes on (it is cheap), so turning it on later gives the end to whoever has earned it, on their next join.

- `/heraldia journal`: one more line before the footer, "Chronicle: guardians defeated", with its bar (it is not one more kind in the overall total: it counts fights, not finds); hovering lists the guardians still keeping watch.
- `/heraldia journal chronicle`: the guardians defeated and those still keeping watch, by their structure's name (never where), the optional ones beyond the map and whether the Chronicle is closed; once closed, `/heraldia journal chronicle book` (a button) gives an up-to-date copy of the last leaf in place of the one carried. The standard is not given again.
- Cost: nothing runs per tick. It hangs from the guardian's death (the same `AFTER_DEATH` as [Cleared uniques](#cleared-uniques)) and from a player joining: it reads about twenty advancements from memory. Measured on the test server: 0.3 ms per death, 1.5-5.9 ms when it gives something (book, banner and advancements).
- Saved: on each player, the guardians they took part in (attachment `heraldia:cronica`, kept on death); on the main world, the once-per-world guardians fallen (`heraldia:cronica_mundo`). What was given is the advancement itself.
- To test: `/advancement grant <player> only heraldia:unicas/<id>/guardian` for each one and rejoin, or kill a guardian with `/damage <guardian> 1000 minecraft:player_attack by <player>`. Tests: `EndingTest` and `JournalTest` (JUnit), `test_chronicle_ending.py` (texts and pages fit in the 8 languages) and the Chronicle part of `tools/smoke/journal.py` (server).

## Herald's Guide

A vanilla written book, the **Herald's Guide**, that explains the mod to players and keeps their diary. Every player gets it **once**, the first time they join after Heraldia is installed or updated (players who already existed in the world too; the version they received is stored on the player and kept on death). It is turned off with `guia.entregar_al_entrar` (see [Configuration](CONFIGURATION.md#configuration)), and any player can ask for an up-to-date copy with `/heraldia guide`.

- **What it covers** (21 pages): a welcome; the villages and their life (trades, job board, cartographers); quests and reputation; the roads; caravans; medium structures; wizard's huts; the mines; lighthouses and wrecks; unique structures; Atlantis; the corsair's isle; the wave arena; how advancements, trophies, boss fights (phases, reinforcements and the boss bar), treasure maps, the treasure hunt and the Chronicle of the Heralds work.
- **Your diary**: how many of this world's unique structures you have discovered (from the "discover" advancements, `heraldia:unicas/<id>/descubrir`) and how many are left, the discovered ones in alphabetical order and, for each missing one, a soft biome-and-feature clue (the same as its Chronicle leaf), **without coordinates** or its name. It counts the unique structures enabled in the configuration plus those you already discovered even if they were disabled later. The diary is a snapshot of the moment it is handed out: ask for a new copy to bring it up to date.
- **Language**: pages are translatable texts whose fallback is in the client's language (any of the mod's 8 languages, chosen by the client's language: `fr_ca` gets French, `pt_pt` Brazilian Portuguese, `es_mx` Spanish; English for the rest), so a player **without the mod** reads it in their language.
- Vanilla book limits: the generator checks that every page fits the 14 lines of a book page (with the same width calculation as the Chronicle leaves), that the title is at most 32 characters and that the book never exceeds 100 pages (with today's 20 unique structures, 23 to 29). It carries `custom_data` `{heraldia_guia_libro:"<version>"}` and the mod edition in its lore.

Cost: it only works when a player without this version's guide joins or when it is asked for (it reads their discovery advancements and builds the book; measured on the test server, see `tools/smoke/guide.py`); nothing per tick.

## Your journal: `/heraldia journal`

Each player's own record of **what they have found and what is still missing**, in chat (vanilla clients too, in the player's language, the same as the job board), for any player and without permissions:

- `/heraldia journal`: the title, a bar with the **overall total** (everything found out of everything that can be found in this world) and one line per kind with its progress bar (■ found, □ missing; gold and ✔ when complete) and its count: **village families**, **unique structures**, **medium structures** (by type), **mine entrances** (by size), the **coast** (lighthouse and shipwreck) and the **Nether** and the **End** (their unique structure and their medium types). Each line is a button that opens its kind; hovering over it lists what is missing.
- `/heraldia journal <kind>` (`villages`, `uniques`, `mediums`, `mines`, `coast`, `nether`, `end`): the names already found and the ones still missing, in alphabetical order of the player's language (mines, by size), with a button back to the summary.
- **No coordinates and no clues**: the journal says "2 of 21 unique structures, these ones are missing", never where they are. Finding them is for the maps, the quests and the Chronicle; the soft biome clues of the missing unique structures stay in the Herald's Guide, which the journal points to (a button to `/heraldia guide`) while any is missing.
- **Where the data come from**: the player's own advancements, which already record every discovery (`aldeas/<family>`, `unicas/<id>/descubrir`, the criteria of `medianas/todas`, `medianas/nether` and `medianas/end`, `minas/<size>`, `costa/faro` and `costa/naufragio`); which ids belong to each kind is also read from the advancements (the criteria of `aldeas/todas`, `unicas/todas` and the three walker advancements) and, for the dimension of a unique structure, from the structure registry. Nothing is saved and nothing runs until someone asks: it reads the player's progress from memory, on the server thread, and answers in the same tick.
- What counts: what can still appear in this world (a kind disabled in the configuration is not "missing") and whatever the player already found, even if it was disabled later. A kind with nothing to find is left out.
- **The end of the Chronicle**: one more line, the guardians defeated towards the [end of the Chronicle](#end-of-the-chronicle), and its own section, `/heraldia journal chronicle`.
- **Why a command and not more Guide pages or `/heraldia status`**: the Guide is a book handed out on joining (it only gets updated when asked for again, and its pages must fit a measured size), and it already keeps the diary of unique structures with their clues; `/heraldia status` is the server's summary, what anyone has discovered. The journal is the per-player view of every kind, always up to date; `/heraldia status` links to it and it links to the Guide, instead of repeating either.

## Filler loot: no empty chest

- **Empty on purpose**, only 13: the Herald's House **return barrels** (12; the market keeps a former tenant's things in a free barrel, and a barrel is free only while it is empty) and the dispenser inside the palace's throne column (a part of the secret door's circuit, which would spit its loot out).

## Restocking loot and difficulty

Two optional server-side features (vanilla clients are enough). Both also work in an **existing world**, on what is already generated.

**Restocking loot** (`botin.reponer.activado`, **off by default**, so turning it on is your decision): chests and barrels of Heraldia villages, medium structures, mine entrances and unique structures are refilled from their loot table again when, **on opening**, all of these are true:

- at least `botin.reponer.dias` in-game days (24,000 ticks of the world clock, the one `/time add` moves; sleeping through the night counts) have passed since it was last looted;
- no other player is within 32 blocks (the one opening it does not count);
- it is empty or almost empty (at most 3 occupied slots out of 27). Nothing is ever deleted: the loot only goes into free slots, so whatever a player stored there stays.

How a chest is recognised: when a Heraldia chest that still has its loot table is opened, the mod stores in the block, as `custom_data` `heraldia_botin: {tabla, saqueado}`, its table and the clock of that looting (nothing else). A chest **already looted before the option was turned on** no longer has its table: the first time it is opened, the mod looks up the Heraldia structure that has a piece there (the chunk's structure references, like `/locate`), and in that piece's template, with its rotation, the loot table it put in that position; from then on it counts as looted at that moment. Lazy: it only works when a player uses a container, never per tick or scanning the world. The premium chest of a unique whose guardian has fallen restocks with a modest table ([below](#premium-chest-of-a-cleared-unique)). Not restocked: the Atlantis premium chest (fixed contents, it has its own option `unicas.atlantida.cofre_unico_por_jugador`), trophies, vanilla tables and other mods' tables, and containers of non-template pieces (the Atlantis base) that were looted before turning it on. With **Lootr** installed it does nothing (detected at startup; Lootr already turns loot chests into per-player chests); the log says so if the option is on.

**Difficulty** (`dificultad.nivel`): scales the health and melee damage of the [guardians](#guardian-trophies) and the template mobs of Heraldia (those tagged `heraldia_anclado` or `heraldia_guardian`, and the bosses' reinforcements, `heraldia_refuerzo`; hostile only: cats and wolves are untouched; spawner mobs are not affected).

| Level | Health | Damage | Loot (`dificultad.escalar_botin`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `facil` | x0.75 | x0.75 | x0.75 |
| `normal` (default) | as always | as always | as always |
| `dificil` | x1.5 | x1.25 | x1.25 |
| `heroica` | x2 | x1.5 | x1.5 |

- They are attribute modifiers with their own ids (`heraldia:dificultad_vida`, `heraldia:dificultad_dano`, `heraldia:dificultad_jugadores`, `add_multiplied_total`), added on top of whatever else the mob has (the effects of the boss phases, other mods) without touching it. They are set every time the mob loads (also the mobs already generated), so after changing the option and restarting the server they are recomputed; setting them twice changes nothing, and with `normal` they are removed (exactly the previous behaviour). The current health keeps its proportion (a guardian at half health stays at half).
- `dificultad.escalar_por_jugadores` (off): +25 % health per extra player within 32 blocks (up to +100 %), recomputed when the mob takes a hit.
- Loot: only plain stackable items (no name or other components) of Heraldia chest tables change their amount (the fractional part with its probability, never below 1 or above a stack). Skeleton arrows and other ranged damage do not change.

Cost: an event when a block is used (leaves at once if it is not a container with a table, or the option is off), on each entity load and on each hit (leaves at once if the mob has no Heraldia tag); measured with `tools/smoke/loot_restock.py` and `tools/smoke/difficulty.py`.

### Premium chest of a cleared unique

With its guardian dead and its spawners off, a unique would otherwise give its **premium chest** again every few days with no fight at all. So, once the guardian of a unique has fallen, its premium chest (table `heraldia:unicas/<id>/premium`) keeps restocking, but with a **modest table** (`heraldia:botin/premium_vencido`) instead of the premium one. Option `botin.reponer.premium_vencido_modesto` (on by default; `false` restocks it with its full premium as before).

- **What a refill brings** (each refill, independently): **1-2 emeralds 40 % of the time** and **one modest enchanted book 8 % of the time** (enchanting-table enchantments at levels 5-15, the like of Sharpness I-II or Efficiency II: never Mending or another treasure enchantment). About half the refills bring nothing. Over a month of refills every 7 days that is about 2-3 emeralds and, with luck, one book: a small thank-you, not a farm. No netherite, elytra, diamonds or named items.
- **The first time is untouched**: the premium of a unique is the one it always was while its guardian lives, and the first opening of the chest (the reward for beating the structure) always comes from the real premium table. Only the refills after the guardian has fallen change. Refills while the guardian still lives are the full premium, as before: the structure is still dangerous.
- **Only the premium chest**: the other chests of a cleared unique (armouries, libraries, cellars, treasure rooms) restock with their own tables, like village and medium chests, which are never guarded either. Their loot is ordinary; the premium is the only one meant as the prize of the fight.
- **"Cleared"** is the same record as [Cleared uniques](#cleared-uniques) (attachment `heraldia:unicas_vencidas`): the start of `heraldia:unicas/<id>` that reaches the chest, recorded when its guardian dies, also with `jefes.apagar_generadores` off. A unique whose guardian died before this record existed (see "In an existing world" there) is not known as cleared and keeps restocking its full premium.
- **Reversible**: the chest keeps its premium table in its record (`heraldia_botin`); the modest table is only used for that refill. Turning the option off gives the full premium again from the next refill. The modest table does not count for the treasure advancement of the unique.
- **Cost**: one structure lookup when a premium chest is due to refill (not on other chests, nor on every opening).
- Test: `ClearedPremiumTest` (JUnit: which tables are premium, which table a due chest gets with the guardian alive or fallen and with the option on or off, that the modest table is never recorded as the chest's own, the generated table's contents and that every unique's premium chest in the templates is recognised).

## Wave arena

A coliseum near spawn, one per world, to play in waves with your friends. Server-side only (vanilla clients are enough).

**What it looks like.** 57 x 57 blocks: the ring (radius 14) sunk 7 blocks below the ground, with a sandstone floor, sea lanterns and seven spawn points in front of barred cells; the ring wall, topped with glass up to an invisible barrier roof (neither arrows nor mobs reach the stands, nobody climbs out and spiders stay in); four tiers of seats; the outer promenade at ground level, open on all sides, with 16 pillars, arches and lanterns; and to the south the entrance: a ramp from the promenade down to the ring, closed by a barred gate while a match lasts. Next to the entrance, inside the ring, the bell.

**Matches.** Any player steps into the ring and rings the bell: the gate closes and a 5 s countdown with titles starts; whoever is in the ring when it ends plays. Each wave brings more and stronger enemies (4 in the first and +50 % per wave; +10 % health and damage per wave: enemies grow faster than their strength). The types rotate: zombies, skeletons and spiders, a mix with creepers and a hard mix (husks, strays, cave spiders, vindicators and witches), then again. Every 5 waves a named mini-boss also comes (Gorbak the Bonebreaker, Black Arrow, the Arena Executioner and the Last Champion, in turn) with the [phases, bar and reinforcements of the guardians](#guardian-fights). At most `arena.max_mobs_vivos` (20) alive at once: if the wave brings more, they come in batches. 10 s pause between waves. `dificultad.nivel` is respected (the arena's modifiers, `heraldia:arena_*`, add up with those of the difficulty and the bosses). A boss bar shows the wave and the enemies left; the sidebar (objective `heraldia_arena`, only during the match and if nobody uses the sidebar) keeps each player's record and the server's, and breaking the server record is announced in chat. The match ends when every participant has fallen or left the ring (`/heraldia arena leave` takes you out); if the server stops or crashes in the middle of one, the arena is clean next time (no enemies, gate open).

**Loot and death.** Enemies drop no items and give `arena.experiencia_porcentaje` % (15) of the normal experience. Rewards come from waves: every 5 cleared, items for each participant (better at 10, 15 and from 20 on: ingots, diamonds, golden apples, enchanted gear; no netherite or elytra), and advancements for clearing waves 5, 10 and 20. If you fall in the ring during a match, **you keep your inventory and experience** and respawn at the entrance.

**Modes** (T41). Ringing the bell offers one option per enabled mode [in the middle of the screen](#in-the-middle-of-the-screen), each with its help (`arena.modos.*`; with only one, it starts right away); picking one starts it as `/heraldia arena start coop|timeattack|survival` would, which any player can still use from the ring (operators and the console, from anywhere). A mode disabled after the menu opened, a match already started or a player who left the ring is refused. All three keep the usual rules: no drops, 15 % experience, inventory kept, a mini-boss every 5 waves, difficulty and profile.
- **Co-op** (`coop`): the usual mode, unchanged: endless waves with a 10 s pause; the score is the wave cleared, and its record stays in the sidebar (`heraldia_arena`).
- **Time attack** (`timeattack`): `arena.modos.oleadas_contrarreloj` waves (10) against the clock, with a 5 s pause; the match ends on its own after the last one and the score is the time (from the first wave to the last one cleared; the countdown does not count). The boss bar shows the wave, the enemies left and the time.
- **Survival** (`survival`): waves with no break (the next one starts right after the previous is cleared) until everyone falls; the score is the highest wave cleared.

**Weekly leaderboard and hall of fame.** Each participant's result (waves cleared and time) goes into the week's leaderboard of its mode when the match ends for them (when they fall or leave, or at the end): one entry per player, the best; more waves first, then less time, then earlier. `/heraldia arena leaderboard [mode [week]]` (anyone) shows the top 3 of each mode (or the top 10 of one) and the all-time record; `week` is how many weeks ago (`0`, the current one). The week closes on `arena.temporada.dia_reinicio` at `arena.temporada.hora_reinicio` (Monday 00:00 by default, server time; if the server was off then, on the next start) or with `/heraldia arena season reset` (operators; `/heraldia arena season` shows the current week and the next reset): its champions are announced in chat, get their advancement (those offline, when they join) and go up on the **hall of fame**: three crimson signs (they do not burn) on the promenade next to the entrance, one per mode, and two pages of the Herald's Guide (champions of the previous week and records). The last `arena.temporada.historico` closed weeks (8) are kept in the world. Six advancements: finish a 10-wave time attack, do it in under 15 minutes, clear wave 15 in survival and win a week in each mode (112 in the tab). **In an existing world**: an arena placed before gets its signs if their spot (in the arena's plan) is empty and has a floor; if something else is there, it is left alone. The previous record of the sidebar becomes the co-op record (once, on the first start with this version).

**Protection.** Nobody but operators can break, place or use the arena's blocks (except the bell) or use buckets near it, and during a match ender pearls cannot be thrown in the ring. Explosions (creepers, TNT, fireballs), pistons and endermen do not touch it, and fire does not catch on it (all its materials are stone, glass or metal). Only inside its box; outside, everything is normal.

**Placing it** (operators, level 2):

- `/heraldia arena place`: finds a flat, dry site with no structures already generated (villages, mine entrances, unique structures, other mods'; underground ones do not count) 200-300 blocks from the world spawn (without loading or generating chunks), gives its coordinates and marks its corners and centre with particle columns for 5 minutes. `/heraldia arena place confirm` builds it: **it clears and levels the ground** (whatever is on top, builds included, is lost), so **make a backup of the world first**. It is built in parts, one chunk per tick (about 3 s).
- `/heraldia arena move` and `mover confirmar`: the same, removing the old one. `/heraldia arena remove` and `quitar confirmar`: removes it (the ground stays flat).
- `/heraldia arena start [coop|timeattack|survival]` and `/heraldia arena stop`: start (co-op without a mode) or stop a match.
- `/heraldia arena season` and `season reset`: the current week and its next reset; close it now (champions, signs and advancements).
- `/heraldia arena` (anyone): where it is, whether there is a match, and the records.

In a **new world** it is built on its own on the first start (`arena.generar_en_mundos_nuevos`); in an **existing world**, only if an operator places it. Cost: with no match, build or proposal, nothing per tick (three checks); protection, an in-memory AABB per block with an early exit when there is no arena; a match, one check per second of its participants and enemies.

## The Herald's House

"La Casa del Heraldo": the world-spawn town, one per world, in the overworld (generator `tools/gen/herald_house.py`, Java `herald/`). Like the arena it is not a world-generation structure: the mod places its template (`heraldia:casa_heraldo/casa`, 128 × 34 × 128 since version 2; the first release's was 96 × 21 × 96) and the blueprint the Java side reads (`heraldia/casa_heraldo.json`) says where everything is.

- **Where.** In a **new world** it builds itself on the first start (`casa_heraldo.generar_en_mundos_nuevos`), at most 48 blocks from spawn (the site with the fewest water samples, then the smallest drop, then the closest), and the world spawn moves to its plaza. In an **existing world**, never on its own: an operator runs `/heraldia herald place` (search near spawn) or `/heraldia herald place here` (centred on them), sees the corners of the footprint and of the protected ring marked with columns of light for five minutes and confirms with `/heraldia herald place confirm`; the world spawn is not touched (the message offers the `/setworldspawn` to type). The decision is kept in the new attachment `heraldia:casa_heraldo`, so a world is never decided twice. It never overlaps the arena (each search avoids the other).
- **Terrain.** A chunk per tick (`herald/Building`): above the footprint, air up to the top of the terrain; under it, a stone foundation down to the ground (up to 48 blocks, over water or a hollow); in the 8 blocks around it, the terrain blended from the town's ground to the natural height, each column keeping the land's own ground (sand on sand, terracotta on badlands, grass only on grassland), and the forecourt outside the south wall takes the commonest of them where it is bare (`Building.exterior`, the blueprint's `exterior`): the town does not lay a green rectangle on a desert. Under the town's outermost four columns the foundation is the wall's own masonry, not bare stone, for where the town stands over lower land. The roads leave through the four gates as dirt paths across the protected ring with a signpost at the end (only with `casa_heraldo.caminos.activado` off: on, the gates' roads below are the road and carry that sign by their end); the first gate that meets water within 32 blocks gets a stone **dock** (`heraldia:casa_heraldo/muelle`) instead.
- **Water.** Once every chunk is placed, no still water is left standing in the air in the footprint or its margin (`herald/Shores`: a small pond the town cut open is drained, a larger body gets a bank), and no running water is left without a source (`Shores.dry`, a step per chunk after those): what had already run out of a drained pond would otherwise stay for good, since the build takes water away without block updates, and the gates' roads would lay walkways over it.
- **Inside** (version 2). A wall four blocks thick with a walk between battlements and a parapet lit by lanterns, four corner towers rising above it (a stair up to the walk, copper cones, banners, lightning rods; their ground entrance is a fence gate, so players go up and villagers do not), arched gates north, west and east, and in the south the **gatehouse**: the arch between two towers (the guardhouse and the watch's storehouse) opening onto a forecourt with the approach avenue, banner poles and flower beds. Two iron golems keep the **night watch** on the walk (golems open no doors). The **plaza**: a raised dais with steps under a copper canopy holds the bell; the **clock tower** (16 blocks to the cornice, a copper spire, a real clock in a frame on each face) shelters the villages' **job board** (`board.piece`, the same lectern and bailiff) in its loggia; the **monument to the Herald**, flagpoles with the town's banner, lamp posts, benches; its middle 23 × 23 is laid out like a village square so the **village events** (`events/`) can happen there. **Each building has its own recipe** (`tools/gen/herald_house.py`): the two-storey **inn** (tuff and calcite with crimson beams, overhanging the plaza on corbels; the hearth, long tables with mugs, the bar and its kegs, the kitchen; the card room, the store and the parlour upstairs), the **town hall** (two storeys, the council hall, the archive upstairs, the balcony over the plaza), the **chapel** (buttresses, rose window, stained glass, pews, the altar, the vestry, a belfry spire), the **library** (bookshelf walls, two lecterns, a reading nook), the **smithy** (forge with a lava cauldron and a smoking chimney, anvils, the coal bin, the anvil yard with the grindstone and a weapon rack), the **butcher** (a red-and-white awning, meat hanging in frames, the tannery), the **cartographer** (maps, compass and spyglass in frames), the **bakery** (the oven), the **herbalist** (shelves of potted herbs), the **workshop** (fletcher and mason), the **weaver** (cloth, banners, the sheep's pen), the **stable** (two horses and a donkey in their stalls), the **granary** by the farm, and the guardhouse. **18 villagers** live there (one workstation and one bed each, no spare bed: a free bed lets them breed and a child takes the nearest free bed, perhaps a resident's; on the test seed 17 or 18 of the 18 hold their bed at night, as vanilla wandering may leave one out); every house has a bell on a post a few steps from its door, so that each resident's bed, workstation and meeting point are a short walk apart. Also the market (8 stalls and the office with 12 return barrels), the **Nether portal** (an obsidian frame, built and not lit, under a pavilion), the farm and allotments, gardens, the watch's training yard (dummies, the archery butt), a chicken run, laundry lines, a cart and the mason's yard (the villages' work-in-progress traces, `traces.py`, built with fireproof materials), cats on the roofs, smoke from the chimneys (lit campfires). Its villagers talk like a village's (`dialogue/`, style `llanuras_castellana`).
- **Relief, texture and skyline** (`tools/gen/herald_dress.py`, over the plain build and adding only blocks: the same entities, block entities and light sources). The wall: a rough plinth, a pilaster every 8 blocks carried up into a taller merlon, merlons two wide between them, a corbel course under the battlements, an arrow loop in each bay outside and a blind arch in each bay inside with a buttress at each pilaster's foot, moss low and cracks high in patches, and two **mural towers** on each side (7 × 5, the walk passing through their room, a tiled hip roof), each standing only where the ground inside is free, 4 blocks from every building and off every door's front. The gatehouse towers: machicolations, string courses, arrow loops and a tiled roof inside the parapet; a keystone over the arch. Every roof: eaves and ridge in a kin material, a few kin blocks in patches, dormers on the long gables and a small raised lantern on the wide hips.
- **Nothing burns.** Building materials are stone, brick, mud brick, terracotta, deepslate, tuff, calcite, copper (waxed), blackstone and crimson wood (fireproof); no oak or other flammable wood anywhere. The few flammable blocks a house needs (bookshelves, carpets, wool, the lecterns, hay, the composters, the coal) are only allowed where `fire_errors` proves that a fire started by lightning cannot reach them (a model of 26.3's `LightningBolt.spawnFire` and `FireBlock.tick`: where a bolt can light fire, and how far fire spreads). Generation fails otherwise.
- **Checked as it is generated** (`herald_house.errors`): every door has 3 free, walkable blocks straight out of it and no other building within 8 blocks in front of it; any two buildings stand at least 4 blocks apart; every door can be walked to from the plaza and the path laid from it reaches a street (`placement_errors`; the first release's layout fails it: its inn opened one block from the smithy's wall); each resident starts next to a workstation of its own, has a free bed at most 16 blocks' walk from it and a bell at most 24 blocks' walk from both, walked as a villager (no ladders, gates shut), no villager can reach the wall walk and no fence can trap one (`resident_errors`).
- **Versions.** The blueprint (`heraldia/casa_heraldo.json`) carries the earlier versions under `anteriores`, and the attachment `heraldia:casa_heraldo` records which version a world placed (`version`; absent means 1). A world that placed the first release's 96 × 96 town keeps it exactly as it is, with its protection, stalls and barrels where they were; only a town placed from now on is version 2.
- **Protection** (`herald/Grounds`): the footprint and **50 blocks** around it (228 × 228 for version 2; 196 × 196 for version 1), from bedrock to the sky. No player who is not an operator breaks, places or uses blocks there (doors, trapdoors, gates, buttons, beds, the bell and the workstations whose screen changes nothing are usable; the job board's book is shown in chat instead of opening the lectern), empties buckets, lights the portal ("only an operator can light it"), hits a villager, the golem or an animal or touches item frames and armour stands. Explosions, pistons, endermen placing blocks and dispensers' buckets go through the arena's mixins, which also ask the house; trampling the farm (`HeraldFarmlandMixin`), endermen picking up blocks near the area (`HeraldEndermanMixin`) and monsters spawning in the area (`HeraldSpawnMixin`) are stopped too.
- **Look, do not take** (`casa_heraldo.contenedores_solo_ver`, on): the chests, barrels, kegs, dispensers, hoppers, furnaces, brewing stands, crafters and shulker boxes of the area (the town and its ring, the grounds' houses too) open for anyone, and a player who is not an operator sees what they really hold (their loot table rolls as usual) but cannot take or put anything: the window is marked look-only when it opens (`HeraldOpenMenuMixin`, also for a chest minecart or a double chest that is half in the area) and every packet that would change it is dropped before vanilla reads it (`HeraldLookPacketMixin`: every input of the container click, the menu buttons, the recipe book's placement, the crafter's slot toggles and the bundle's selection), with the window and the inventory sent back as they are and the line "you can look, but not take anything". A creative pick-block with data (Ctrl) of a protected block copies it without its contents. Still theirs as before: an operator, a tenant in their own stall's box, the owner of a return barrel (the market keeps filling and freeing them) and the ender chest. A container in a stall someone else rents stays shut. Lecterns are still read in chat (their book cannot be taken); signs, flower pots, cauldrons, composters, jukeboxes, chiseled bookshelves, decorated pots and shelves stay unusable, as using them takes or changes something. Off, the containers cannot be opened, as before.
- **Market stalls for rent** (`herald/Market`, off by default: `casa_heraldo.mercado.activado`). A click on a stall's sign shows its state with buttons. `/heraldia herald stall claim <n>` takes a free stall for the entry fee plus the first period (in emeralds), `pay <n> [periods]` pays ahead (up to 4 periods from now; in the grace it pays the arrears first), `leave <n>` gives it up, `/heraldia herald stall` lists them, and operators `evict <n>`. One stall per player. Inside their own stall (a 3 × 3 × 3 box) the tenant may do anything. Rent is charged **lazily** from the world's game time (it only runs while the server does): looked at on every click and command, when the tenant joins and once a minute while there are tenancies; never a countdown per tick. The tenant is warned a day before the rent is due and again in the grace (`casa_heraldo.mercado.gracia_dias`). Once the grace ends the stall is reclaimed and **nothing is destroyed**: blocks, container contents, item frames, armour stands, paintings and loose items go into a free return barrel of the market office assigned to the tenant (only they and operators open it) and they are told where it is (then, or on their next join). If the barrels have no room, nothing is touched and the stall stays closed until an operator empties one. With the market turned off, nothing is charged or reclaimed. Prices are options (`/heraldia config set casa_heraldo.mercado.<option> <value>`).
- **Cost.** One field check per tick while nothing is being built or proposed; the market's check once a minute (`getTickCount() % 1200`) and only with tenancies; protection checks are an early exit on one field and integer comparisons. Building it takes about 315 ticks (one chunk, or one road, per tick; 6-9 s of server thread in all, against 205 ticks for the first town), plus one tick to set its beds again (a bed across a chunk border is lost when its chunks are placed one by one). Entities (version 2): 18 villagers and the bailiff (no AI), 3 iron golems, 3 chickens; without AI (they cost almost nothing to tick): 2 horses, a donkey, 2 sheep, 2 cats; 3 practice dummies and the Herald's statue (armour stands) and 52 item frames. Play cost: see [Play cost](DEVELOPMENT.md#play-cost-toolssmokeruntime_costpy), scenario `casa_heraldo`.

### The Herald's House roads

Generator `tools/gen/spawn_roads.py` (layout, huts, blueprint `heraldia/caminos_casa.json`, texts), Java `herald/SpawnRoads` (the build), `roads/SpawnRoadLayout` (the layout, the same arithmetic as the generator's) and `roads/BuiltRoads` (paving a chunk that already exists like `roads/RoadFeature` paints one being generated).

- **The roads**: from each gate, 5 wide, straight for 44 blocks through the protected ring and then a gentle bend to its end 72 blocks out (at least 45: the owner's minimum), up to 10 to one side from the seed. Built as one work (`roads/BuiltRoads`, `roads/RoadProfile`): one paving for the whole width with a kerb on each edge (a dirt path with coarse dirt edges; smooth sandstone with cut sandstone on sand, red on red sand and terracotta; gravel with cobblestone on stone and snow; packed mud with mud bricks), chosen along the road by the ground most of the 17 blocks around have, so it changes at one line across it and never in loose patches. Level across its width and smoothed along it (the mean of the natural ground 5 blocks before and after, under any tree), climbing a slope in regular one-block steps; the ground is cut or filled for it (at most 10 blocks). Four blocks of air over it: every trunk, leaf, plant or bump of ground in that space goes, and a tree standing on the road or its shoulder is felled whole, its leaves too (none is left hanging). The shoulder, a block beyond each edge, is brought within a block of the road keeping its grass or sand, and cleared of trees and leaves 3 blocks up; its flowers stay. Lamp posts stand beside the road, never on it: on the shoulder, one block further out, on a cobblestone footing in water up to 6 deep, and only by deeper water on the road's kerb or a bridge's edge. Leaves left hanging near the road by anything that cut a tree without felling it (the town's blended margin, a hut's site) are taken away as their decay would. Plank bridges and walkways over short water. A column that is not natural ground (a player's block, a structure's piece, the town, the arena) is left as it is. A sign by the end of each gate's road points back to the Herald's House with its distance. Lamp posts (two fence posts of the biome's wood and a lantern) every 7 blocks, alternating sides, one at each end and one outside every sharp bend: every road column stays within 10 blocks (Manhattan) of a lantern, so its block light is at least 1 and no monster spawns on it (checked as a property over many seeds in `tests/test_spawn_roads.py`, and on the real build, which logs how many road columns outside the protected ring are dark).
- **The arena road**: the gate whose road points closest to the arena (its centre), that road continued from its end (or from the end of its straight stretch, if its bend would come too close to the arena) to the arena's entrance on the south side of its box, through the shortest of a few detours (to either side, or round the arena's corners) that keeps 3 blocks from the arena's box and 8 from the town, and, if one exists, out of every chunk with a surface structure (villages, mines, medium and unique structures: read from the saved chunks, off the server thread); its last 10 blocks come straight out of the entrance. A sign at each end (to the arena, to the Herald's House, with the distance). An arena more than 420 blocks from the road's end is not joined.
- **The huts**: 7 × 7 (a 2-block porch and the hut), each its own building under the same plan (`spawn_roads.LOOK`): plank, log-cabin, white daub, mud brick, stone, brick or cobble walls on a footing; a gable along the road, a gable end facing it, a hip or a single slope; a striped wool awning or a slab porch on posts, always open over the door (the door rule only checks a door whose way out is under the sky); something of the trade on the porch (hay, a target, wool, dried kelp, an anvil) and, on two, a chimney stack. They stand beside the straight stretch, door to the road and the bell on the porch floor beside it; inside, the bed, the workstation of its villager's profession (vanilla trades) and a lantern. Places: the guide's on the right of the way out, 8 blocks from the gate; then 16 on the left and 26 on the right of every road. `casa_heraldo.caminos.aldeanos` villagers in all (5: the guide and one per road; at most 8), one per hut, filled road by road (north, east, south, west) and slot by slot; with no guide yet one place is kept for it. A site under water, too steep (more than 4 blocks) or on a structure is skipped. The door rule (`doors.py`) is mandatory for them: their templates, and their places beside the four roads with the town (`spawn_roads.layout_errors`).
- **The guide** (Garcés, warden of the road; a weaponsmith): a click opens his conversation in the choice window (the mod's screen, or the container menu on a vanilla client; never the chat, whatever `dialogos.en_pantalla` says): the road leads to the arena where the finest warriors fight, how far it is, and, with [Tell me more], a little of its story; [Show me your wares] opens his trades (sneak + click trades at once).
- **When**: right after a town is placed (`/heraldia herald place confirm`, or on its own in a new world); the arena road then too if the arena stands, otherwise when the arena is placed. In a world whose town came before this feature, never on its own: `/heraldia herald roads` (operators) builds what is missing. What was built is saved (`heraldia:casa_heraldo_caminos`): nothing is built twice.


### The Herald's House grounds

Generator `tools/gen/herald_grounds.py` (the houses, their slots and the cherry sites, blueprint `heraldia/alrededores_casa.json`, texts), Java `herald/OutskirtsPlan` (the plan: no game classes, tested with made-up terrain) and `herald/Outskirts` (the build).

- **What**: the protected ring around the town (50 blocks on each side of the 128 × 128 town, about 35,600 columns) levelled into gentle ground, with a few cherry trees and a few small houses. Not one flat plateau: the ground keeps the shape of the land, smoothed (the mean over a 13 × 13 window), with no two side by side columns more than a block apart, no loose column and no hole; where that smoothed ground would still climb faster than a block every 2.2 columns (a hillside, the slope down from a town on a hill: a "rice terrace" staircase of one-block steps one after another, what the owner found at their spawn), it is gathered instead into **terraces** about 5 columns wide whose risers (2 to 4 blocks) are **retaining walls** of stone faced on their open side (cobblestone with some mossy on grass and dirt, cut and smooth sandstone on sand, their red kind on red sand, mud bricks on mud, stone bricks on stone); next to a road, a hut, a house, the water and the ring's edge the riser shrinks a block per column, so the ground still meets each of them walking; it meets the town's ground at the wall and the natural ground at the ring's outer edge, whose outermost row is left as it is (the natural ground beyond keeps its own steps, so those few rows are counted apart). Each column is cut or filled by at most 10 blocks, the roads' own cap; a narrow pit deeper than that (a cave's mouth, a ravine, a sinkhole: one a 7 × 7 window spans) is covered with a 3-block lid at the height of the ground round it instead of a crater being dug round it, the air under it left as it was. Where a cliff is too high for that, the ground does what the cap allows and keeps a step there, and the log says how many columns were too steep. The shore never goes under the water beside it, and once the ground is levelled no still water is left standing in the air anywhere in the town, its margin or the ring (water placed by world generation has no fluid tick: a pond whose ground was cut away would stand there for good): a small pond left open drains as it would have run out, a bigger body gets a bank of ground where it was open (`herald/Shores`, also run by the town's own build); water, ice, structures, players' blocks, the roads that were built (with their shoulders), their lamp posts, signs and huts are never touched, and the ground is shaped round them. Trees on a levelled column are felled whole (leaves and all); the rest stay.
- **The houses**: five designs, none flammable (white and pink terracotta with a granite roof; bricks with a chimney and a dark tiled roof; mossy stone bricks with a blackstone roof; mud bricks; tuff bricks with a bench in the yard), each with a crimson door opening onto a little front yard with flowers and a lantern post, glass or iron-bar windows, a table and chairs, a crafting table or furnaces, potted plants, a decorated pot and **a chest whose contents come from one of the mod's filler loot tables** (`heraldia:relleno/*`: a few cheap stacks, never empty, drawn when it is first opened, never restocked or scaled by the difficulty). Nothing a villager would claim (no bed, bell or workstation: the town's residents are the entity budget, and a free bed lets them breed) and no entity. Twelve slots in the ring's sides and corners, each with a second place nearby tried when the first does not fit (a lake, a slope, a road), all away from the gates' road corridors, their huts and lamps; `casa_heraldo.alrededores.casas` of them (8 by default, two per side; one house per slot at most), each only where its plot is natural ground the plan leaves flat within 5 blocks; the plot is levelled to one height. The door rule (`doors.py`) is mandatory for them: their templates, and their slots with the town and every roadside hut (`herald_grounds.layout_errors`). The roads never touch a house, and the arena road goes round their chunks as it goes round any structure (tested over many seeds and arena sites in `tests/test_herald_grounds.py`; only when every detour crosses a structure, which happens in about 1 % of worlds, can it reach one, and it leaves those columns as they are).
- **The cherry trees**: up to 10 (vanilla's own cherry tree, with a few pink petals round it), on sites away from the houses, the roads and the town, each only on grass, on gentle ground and with no fire source (lava, fire, a campfire, magma) within 6 blocks. A site that does not fit in a given world is skipped and only counted in the log (3 of 9 on the test seed).
- **Protection**: all of it is inside the protected area, under its rules: a player who is not an operator cannot break or place anything there and, as with every container in the area, can open the houses' chests to look but not take anything.
- **When**: right after a town is placed, once its roads are built (with the roads off, at once). In a world whose town came before this feature, never on its own: `/heraldia herald grounds` (operators). What was built is saved (`heraldia:casa_heraldo_alrededores`): never built twice. Its cost is measured by `tools/smoke/herald_grounds.py` (the build logs it: ticks, server-thread milliseconds and the slowest step, and its audit of every column as it stands).
